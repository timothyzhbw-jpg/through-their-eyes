A teaching scenario (SPEC 3.6). **The look times are scripted for teaching; what he sees, believes and does follows the sim.** His looks (direction from where his body faces, seconds before the planned reception): L 120° at 5.15 s, R -120° at 3.9 s, B 150° at 2.7 s, F -150° at 1.25 s. Rule 1 moves his receiving spot after each look, up to 3 m, away from the opponents he believes will be within 7 m of him when the ball arrives; rule 2 turns him 70° side-on at reception when he believes one is within 6 m. All three runs share every random draw (SPEC 3.7). Generated by `uv run python -m eval.pro_scan`.

## Reading (held-out seeds)

With three looks he receives +1.32 m further from the nearest opponent on average (further in 39/40 runs), and side-on in 35/40 against 0/40 with one look.
His decision barely changes: he plays forward to the striker in 31/40 runs with three looks and 30/40 with one. The forward pass is his highest-valued option in 39/40 and 40/40: on either picture it is the pass he wants, and the decision model's softmax draw (SPEC 3.5) picks another in the rest. What the looks change is how he receives the ball.
His forward passes complete in 31/31 with three looks and 27/30 with one; the mean xT gained is +0.143 against +0.116.
Without the forward look alone he receives where, and as square as, with one look in 40/40 runs: the forward look, which shows the central defender stepping out toward him, is the one the two rules act on.
The first look (left) finds little new: everyone is known at the run's first frame (SPEC 3.4), and the players it shows have barely moved since.

Limits: the look times are scripted for teaching; what he sees, believes and does follows the sim. The sim has no first touch or reception mechanics, so the half-turn changes his body angle and his next action's options (where the ball sits, where his eyes rest while he has it, what an opponent can win ball-first), not the touch.
