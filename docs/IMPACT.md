**Which comparisons hold.** Lower aggregate revenue is not worse management: the Premier League is
open (promotion and relegation, no salary cap), while US leagues are closed, with salary caps or a
luxury tax, and share national money equally (4). Deloitte counts club accounts, European prize money included; US
leagues publish no audited totals, so most US revenue figures are estimates. Per-season rights
values, distribution models (5) and each league's own revenue mix (1, 2) do compare. On those terms
the gap is format, not size: US rights holders sell one game to several audiences through
alternate streams with AI features (5, 6), while about half of the Premier League's rights income
and most of its audience are abroad (2, 3).

## Where Through Their Eyes fits

Through Their Eyes is built for that gap: it explains one moment for five audiences (casual,
analyst, player fan, coach, audio description) in English or Chinese, and delivers its overlays as
timecoded, machine-readable cues that a broadcaster's graphics could render. Code checks every
number in its captions and narration against the match data before it airs, and each caption opens
its evidence: AI output a broadcaster can audit (it catches invented facts, not
omissions). For analysts and coaches it shows what a replay cannot: the player's scans,
who was in his sight and whom he had lost track of, read from positions and head direction alone.
It runs on synthetic matches only, so it shows the workflow; accuracy on real matches would need a
rights holder's tracking data.
