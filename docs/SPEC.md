## 0. Pitch and judging alignment

**One-liner:** See what they saw. An AI studio that explains key moments through the player's own
perception — the scans before the ball arrived, what was visible, what he believed — and proves
every on-screen number before it airs.

**Target prizes:** Grand Prize + Best Multi-Agent System (judges may reassign the category).

**Lead with perception.** Freeze frames, tactical boards, shot probabilities, paced commentary and
even deterministic number checks already appear in other broadcast-hackathon entries (REFERENCES,
"Related hackathon work"). None of them shows what the player saw or what he would have done
without one look. Open every pitch, video and README with perception and the counterfactual;
verified numbers are the backbone, not the headline.
In this competition, number checking, fan/analyst modes and multiple languages are already
standard (REFERENCES, "Competing entries"). What only we have: a perception model with a
first-person view, counterfactuals re-simulated inside the sim, and 25 Hz physical tracking with
head yaw. One entry shows "what he sees" as the passing lanes that existed; we show the lanes he
knew existed.

## 1. Audiences (personas)

| Persona | Gets | Tone |
|---|---|---|
| `casual` | First-person replay + a one-sentence story | plain language, minimal numbers |
| `analyst` | Scan rate vs research benchmarks, time since last scan at reception, observed-space share, belief error, counterfactual delta, pitch-control / pressing overlays | dense, technical |
| `player_fan` | Camera locked on the chosen player all match; his scans before each reception; his best decisions | personal |
| `coach` (stretch) | "Scanned vs didn't scan" side-by-side + one coaching cue | instructional |
| `audio_description` | Blind and low-vision viewers: the moment told in the order it happened — where he got the ball, who he could not see, which way he looked, whom the look showed him, what he did — sound on by default, large-print captions | calm, slower voice at any tier; no xT or probabilities |

All personas render the **same verified fact set**. Persona changes presentation, never facts.
Languages: `en` and `zh` for the MVP; `es` is a stretch goal.

**Latency model.** Live overlays come from deterministic analytics only (target p95 < 100 ms
server-side). AI breakdowns run asynchronously once a moment is detected and are shown in the
replay window (target p95 < 12 s), the way broadcast replays follow a goal. Live play never waits
on an LLM.

*Implemented (`api/`, M3):* FastAPI.
- `WS /ws/matches/<id>/live?speed=1` replays the factual run at 25 Hz with `frame`, `event`,
  `moment` and `end` messages; clients can pause, resume, seek, change speed or stop.
- REST: `/api/matches`, `/api/matches/<id>` (+ `/export`, `/moments`, `/audit`),
  `POST /api/breakdowns` (`fail` for the failure drill, §6),
  `POST /api/breakdowns/<key>/bring-back`, `POST /api/whatif`, `POST /api/tts` (503 until Speech
  is configured), `POST /api/verify` (one claim re-checked with one numeric slot changed; not
  audited, rate-limited per client, no model call).
- The service is public with no login (§8), so it is bounded: `POST /api/tts` takes a voice cue
  key from a breakdown it served, never SSML, and is rate-limited per client; the live stream
  allows a few streams per client and a session length, and closes on a malformed control
  message; request bodies are capped at 16 KiB; personas and languages are deduplicated; responses
  carry security headers, and a 500 carries no traceback.
- Measured locally: 25.0 fps with a p95 frame gap of 41 ms; a cached breakdown is served in about
  1 ms.
- The cache is one JSON file per key under `TTE_CACHE_DIR`; Cosmos DB can replace it (§8).

**Data flow.**
- The sim runs offline and writes match files. `api/` replays a match at 1× wall-clock as the live
  ingest stream (frames + events over WebSocket), so Ingest really happens "as events happen"
  (rules: *Ingest synthetic match events as they happen*) while the sim stays deterministic and cheap.
- Moment detection runs on the live stream. Counterfactuals are re-simulated from snapshots on
  demand, and precomputed for the demo match.
- **Breakdown cache.** Every agent output is cached under a content hash of
  (MomentCard, persona, language, prompt version, model deployment). The public demo serves cached
  breakdowns by default; live regeneration is rate-limited per IP. The demo has no login and must
  stay up until 2026-11-10, so it must survive exhausted model quota or credit.

## 3. Synthetic match engine (`sim/`)

### 3.1 State and schema
- **A match is 15–25 scenario-driven possessions (§3.6) stitched together with restarts**
  (kick-off, throw-in, goal kick, corner): 10–15 min of play in total, not 90 min of free play.
  Making 22 free agents look like football is the biggest time sink, and hero moments must be
  controllable anyway. Rates in the realism report are normalised per 90 min.
  *Implemented (2026-10-20, `sim/match.py`), 5–8 possessions for now:* `match-1` has six — open
  play, `blind_side_run` seed 3, open play, `press_escape` seed 0, open play twice — 132.6 s on one
  clock. Hero possessions keep their demo seeds; open-play seeds are keyed off the match seed and
  start at 1,000,000, so they are never design or eval seeds. A restart follows how the previous
  possession stopped (throw-in, goal kick, corner), or is a `cut` when it ended in play: in
  `match-1` all five are cuts, as no open-play possession put the ball out within its 30 s (with
  60 s, 7 of 30 ended in a throw-in). The score counts `goal` events; with no shot outcomes yet
  (M4) it stays 0–0. Each possession stays its own `MatchRun`: moments, lenses, counterfactuals
  and breakdowns run per possession and are lifted to match time (`explain/match.py`). The API
  serves it as `match-1` (`TTE_MATCHES=…,match:1`); the export is the hero export's run schema
  over the whole clock plus a `match` block.
- Pitch 105 × 68 m, origin at the centre spot; the home team attacks +x in the first half. 25 Hz.
- Per frame, per player: `id, team, role, x, y, vx, vy, body_yaw, head_yaw` (world frame), `has_ball`.
- Ball: `x, y, z, vx, vy, vz, owner`.
- Events: `pass, reception, carry, shot, tackle, interception, pressure, possession_change, out,
  goal, scan, head_turn` with `id, t, frame, player, team, attrs` (e.g. pass start/end, speed,
  outcome, difficulty). Ids are `<type>:<player>:<frame>`, stable across factual and
  counterfactual runs.
- `scan` events are **ground truth** from the sim. The analytics scan detector must recover them
  from tracking data only — `head_yaw` plus player and ball positions — with no access to sim
  scan events or other sim-internal state (§4.7).
- Output: JSONL frames + JSON events. Optional kloppy-compatible export (stretch).

### 3.2 Movement
- Formation anchors (e.g. 4-3-3 vs 4-2-3-1) shifted by ball position and phase; compactness and
  line height are team-style knobs.
- Steering toward targets with limits — *tunable*: max speed by role 8.0–9.5 m/s, max accel
  ≈ 3.5 m/s², max decel ≈ 5 m/s², turn-rate limit.
- Separation steering to avoid overlaps (Python-RVO2 optional, see REFERENCES).
- Pressing: the nearest 1–2 defenders converge on the ball carrier when a team pressing trigger fires.
- Marking (`possession_play`): every other outfield defender takes an attacker in his zone and stays
  goal-side of him, 3–6 m off or, in half of his 10 s spells (keyed draws), tight at 1–2 m. So a
  receiver under tight pressure has usually been marked for a while, and the scan slowdown under
  pressure (§3.4) can show in the realism report. A passer judges a teammate's space without the
  opponents beyond him along the pass, such as a goal-side marker: a ball to his feet gets there first.

### 3.3 Ball physics
- Flight: gravity + quadratic drag — *tunable*: m = 0.43 kg, r = 0.11 m, ρ = 1.2 kg/m³, C_d ≈ 0.25.
  Magnus optional.
- Ground: rolling friction; bounce with restitution ≈ 0.5 (*tunable*).
- Passes and shots solve launch speed/angle to reach the target (ground vs lofted). Shot speeds
  roughly 80–130 km/h by context.
- *M1:* ground passes only, in closed form (rolling resistance 0.6 m/s² + quadratic drag), used
  both for frame stepping and for the decision model's predictions. Longer passes are played to
  arrive faster (arrival speed 0.35 × distance, clipped to 6–12 m/s). Lofted passes, bounce, Magnus
  and shot outcomes are M4.

### 3.4 Perception (the hero feature)
- **Head yaw dynamics.** Baseline gaze tracks the ball. A **scan** is a rapid turn away from the
  ball and back. Most scans are short: >90% last ≤ 0.66 s in eye-tracking work (see REFERENCES).
- **Scan scheduling.** Stochastic process with rate by role and context, plus a per-player trait
  multiplier. Calibration targets from research:
  - ≈ 3 scans in the 10 s before receiving, on average;
  - > 0.5 scans/s counts as very high (elite central midfielders);
  - central midfielders and centre-backs scan most, forwards least;
  - scanning drops under tight pressure.

  *Calibrated values* (`eval/scan_calibration.py`, tuning seeds 0–99; held-out check in
  `eval/reports/realism.md`): each scan turns at a peak speed drawn from 404 ± 51°/s (Kokubu et
  al. 2026); the dwell is lognormal, so 90% of scans keep the ball out of an 82° frame ≤ 0.66 s
  and a few hold the look for up to ~3 s (Aksum et al. 2021); the rate scales with the closest
  opponent's distance, from 0.61 at ≤ 1 m to 1 at ≥ 10 m (Jordet et al. 2020); role rates are
  fitted to ≈ 3 scans in the 10 s before receiving and to Jordet et al.'s role effect sizes. The
  per-player trait multiplier is not in yet.
- **Non-scan head motion (nuisance).** So the scan detector is not graded on a signal we made
  clean ourselves, the sim also produces head motion that is not a scan: fast ball tracking during
  passes, sensor noise (σ ≈ 2°, *tunable*), partial/aborted scans and slow drift. Partial turns
  are `head_turn` events (`attrs.partial = true`), never `scan` events: they are not scans.
- **Field of view** (after Bekkers 2026, *Wide Open Gazes*): base 120° binocular cone around
  `head_yaw`; visibility decays with angular offset and distance (Gaussian); the cone narrows as
  running speed rises.
- **Occlusion.** Other players block angular sectors behind them (soft edges).
- **Belief state (mental map).** For every other player: last-seen position, velocity and time.
  Belief = extrapolation from last sighting; uncertainty grows with time since seen. Updated
  whenever that player falls inside the probabilistic visible region.
  *M1 values:* a player counts as seen when V ≥ 0.5; beliefs are extrapolated at most 1 s;
  position uncertainty = 0.3 m + 1.0 m per second of age (all *tunable*).

### 3.5 Decision model
- The ball carrier evaluates candidate actions (pass to each teammate, carry, shot) **on its
  belief state**, never on ground truth.
- Pass value = P(completion | belief) × ΔxT(target). P comes from interception risk along the ball
  path (defender time-to-intercept vs ball arrival time) and receiver pitch control at the target.
  Implemented as expected threat after the action, P·xT(target) − (1 − P)·xT_opp(target): the
  current xT is the same for every option and drops out of the softmax. P also includes the chance
  that the receiver gets there before the ball has rolled on (0.5 s slack).
- **An opponent can also run into the ball before he reacts** (2026-10-20). TTI (§4.1) has him keep
  his velocity for the reaction time, and its closed form only counts arrivals after it, so a
  presser sprinting at the carrier, or an opponent standing on the passing lane, looked as if the
  ball got past him. Under the same motion model, the decision model also takes the closest
  approach of his reaction-time run to the ball, at the same moment, from t = 0 along the ball's
  path (pass) or the carry (5 m in 1 s); within the sim's control radius (1 m, the radius its
  control and tackle rules use) he wins it. P = logistic of (radius − closest approach) with the
  spread of the belief's position uncertainty (0.3 m + 1 m per second of belief age); no new
  tuned parameter. Per opponent the larger of the two chances counts. The TTI kernel itself
  (pitch control, pressing, shaders) is unchanged. Test: `tests/test_decision_run_in.py`.
- A carry is scored along its path like a pass (TTI at each sample of the path, from 0.08 s to
  1 s). While the carry is no faster than v_max (5 m in 1 s at v_max 5 m/s, as now) the TTI margin
  never falls along it, so the endpoint is its worst point and the result is the same as before.
  *Limit:* after the reaction, TTI is to a point of the path, not to within the 1 m control
  radius, for passes, carries and pitch control alike. An opponent who would step to within 1 m
  of the ball just after his reaction, but not onto its path in time, is under-counted, e.g. one
  standing 1.2 m beside a 5 m carry, 3 m along it, gets P(keep) 0.64, while the sim's tackle rule
  takes the ball from anyone who gets within 1 m of it.
- Each teammate is evaluated at several targets along his believed run (lead 0–2 s); the best one
  is his option. Until the fitted xT grid exists (§4.5), decisions use a smooth xT prior.
- Softmax with a per-player temperature. Receiver body orientation affects completion (facing play
  vs back to goal) — not yet in M1.
- **Sampling is Gumbel-max** with keyed noise per (player, frame, option): argmax(value/T + g) is
  exactly a softmax draw, and in a counterfactual an option whose value did not change keeps its
  noise (a Gumbel-max structural causal model, Oberst & Sontag 2019). A single uniform over a
  cumulative distribution would make the flip depend on option order.
- Consequence: scanning changes information, which changes decisions. This is what makes the
  explanation causal *inside the sim*. Outside the sim (real data) we only claim association.

### 3.6 Scenario templates (hero moments)
Parameterised templates that set up recognisable situations inside normal play: counter-attack
after a turnover, third-man run, switch of play, high-press trap, through-ball behind a high line.
Randomised parameters; the same physics and decision code. Documented in the README as
scenario-driven generation.

**Hero design rule.** In a scan-lens hero scenario, the free option (e.g. the runner) is visible to
the decision-maker only during the key scan, and the last sighting before it shows him marked or in
a low-value position. `drop_scan` then flips the decision because of how the scene is built, not by
chance.

**Pressure hero (`press_escape`, 2026-10-20).** A man-oriented high press: the defensive
midfielder H6 lays the ball back to the centre-back H4 at the corner of his box, which sends A11 at
him; every nearby option is marked tightly except one (H5 or H8, a keyed draw). Design rule: the
presser comes from where H4 is looking, so he needs no scan to see him, and nothing forces,
suppresses or times a scan. Tackles are on in this scenario only (`Scenario.tackles`: an opponent
within the control radius of a carried ball wins it), so the other scenarios keep their runs byte
for byte. Its intervention is `force:carry`: had he kept the ball, would the press have won it?
Acceptance (`eval/hero_lens.py`; designed on seeds 0–99; fresh held-out seeds 200–239, as
100–139 were used to find the cause below):
- the pressure lens is selected in 38/40 (target ≥ 60%: met; median margin 0.102 xT);
- forcing the carry loses the ball in 40/40, and the possession outcome changes in 32/40 (target
  ≥ 60%: met). In the other 8 H4's own pass was intercepted too, none by a carry: 6 long passes
  (32–46 m) he rated 0.24–0.75, cut out 1–2.3 s later by a defender 22–48 m away (the truth
  within 0.11 of his belief), and 2 balls to H9 he rated 0.04, drawn by the softmax with
  probability 0.02 and taken by the presser.
- History: first measured on 100–139 with lens 31/40 and outcome 22/40 (**missed**). In 18 seeds
  H4's own action lost the ball as well: he carried into the press (7) or passed through the
  presser (11). Cause: the decision model's TTI kept a sprinting presser's velocity for the 0.7 s
  reaction time, so it rated a carry, or a pass along the presser's line, as getting past him;
  the sim's tackle and control rules did not. One redesign, tried on design seeds only (pressing
  him 6 m deeper; the presser off the passing lanes), did not help (39/80 kept; or no press at
  all), so the scenario was not tuned further. The fix is in the decision model (§3.5, the run-in), not
  in the scenario; on 100–139 it gives lens 39/40 and outcome 30/40, no carry lost
  ([hero_lens_press_escape_100-139.md](../eval/reports/hero_lens_press_escape_100-139.md)).
  `blind_side_run` is unchanged where it matters: 39/40 flips, its seed 3 byte for byte.

### 3.7 Determinism and counterfactuals
- One match seed. **Randomness is keyed, not sequential:** every draw comes from a generator keyed
  by `(match_seed, entity_id, purpose)` that draws a fixed number of values per frame whatever
  happens in the sim (or, equivalently, a generator keyed by frame as well). A single shared
  stream is not allowed: an intervention such as `drop_scan` would change how many values are
  drawn, shift every later draw, and mix noise into the counterfactual delta. Keyed draws give
  common random numbers — factual and counterfactual runs differ only through the intervention.
- **Byte-identical within one environment.** Same seed + same platform + same numpy version ⇒
  byte-identical output. Serialised output is quantised (positions and velocities to 1 mm and
  1 mm/s, angles to 1e-4 rad). Cross-platform identity is not promised: numpy's SIMD
  transcendental functions can differ in the last bits between ARM and x86, and a multi-agent sim
  amplifies that. CI compares runs inside CI, never against hashes made on a laptop.
- `snapshot(t)` / `resume(snapshot, overrides)` re-simulate from t with overrides such as
  `drop_scan(scan_id)`, `freeze_belief(player, at=t')`, `move_player(...)`, and
  `force_action(player, frame, action)` (*M4*): at that decision the player takes `action` — any
  option he evaluated, e.g. `pass:H4` — instead of sampling; everything else re-simulates as
  usual. It answers "what if he had passed to H4?" with a re-simulation, not a statistic.
- A counterfactual result = alternative decision + outcome + ΔxT / Δ shot probability.

## 4. Analytics kernels (`analytics/`)
Every kernel: numpy reference + unit tests. GLSL versions in `web/` must match the numpy reference
within tolerance on fixed snapshots.

### 4.1 Time to intercept (TTI)
Shaw-style simplification of Spearman: the player keeps current velocity for reaction time τ_r,
then runs at v_max to the target. Defaults (*tunable*): τ_r = 0.7 s, v_max = 5 m/s (a deliberately
conservative model parameter, not real sprint speed). Targets: grid cells (pitch control) and
moving players/ball (pressing). The kernel counts arrivals after the reaction time only; the
decision model adds the run-in during it (§3.5) on top of the kernel, which stays as it is.

### 4.2 Pitch control
Closed-form approximation:
`P_home(x) = 1 / (1 + exp(−π / (√3 · σ) · (T_away(x) − T_home(x))))`,
where `T_team(x)` = min over that team's players of TTI and σ ≈ 0.45 s (*tunable*).
Backend grid 105 × 68 at 1 m; per-pixel in the shader. May refine toward Spearman's full
integration later if time allows.

### 4.3 Pressing intensity (after Bekkers)
Defender→attacker and defender→ball TTI, mapped to a probability with a logistic. Per-frame matrix;
aggregate pressure on the ball carrier.
- `p[d, a] = logistic(π / (√3 σ) · (T − TTI[d, a]))` with T = 1.5 s, σ = 0.45 s (*tunable*).
- The carrier's column is the max of pressure on his body and on the ball.
- Aggregate on a player: `1 − Π_d (1 − p[d, a])`. No pressure while the ball is free.
- Also from the paper: targets 1 s ahead (`r + v`), heading penalty `|v|·β/π`; active filter off.

### 4.4 Vision map and observed-space metrics
Per player: `V(x) = FOV(x; head_yaw, speed) × (1 − Occlusion(x))`. Metrics:
- `observed_defended_share` = Σ V·P_def / Σ P_def over opponent-controlled space — the share of
  the opponents' space he actually saw.
- `time_since_last_scan_at_reception`, `scans_in_window(t−10 s, t)`.
- `belief_error`: mean distance between believed and true positions of the relevant players.
- **Blind-side alert** (*M4*, live): perception made visible during play, not only in replays.
  Computed per frame from tracking only — observed `head_yaw` plus positions, the same inputs as
  the scan detector (§4.7), never the sim's internal beliefs — so it would run on real skeletal
  tracking. For an observer `o` and a target `t`, the alert is on when:
  - `t` has been outside `o`'s visible region (V < 0.5, §4.4) for ≥ 1.0 s;
  - `t` has moved ≥ 3 m since `o` last saw him, at ≥ 4 m/s;
  - `t` is within 35 m of `o` (25 m missed H9, who runs 22–30 m from H8 in `blind_side_run`).
  Observers are the ball carrier and the intended receiver (targets: teammates making runs) and
  the four defenders nearest the ball (targets: opponents). Hysteresis: on for at least 0.4 s; it
  clears when `o` sees `t` again. All thresholds are *tunable*; the values above are the starting
  point, and the tuned defaults and their tables are in `eval/reports/blind_side.md`
  (`analytics/blind_side.py`, M4). Deterministic, no LLM, within the
  live overlay budget (§2). An alert that ends in a pass to its target within 3 s is a moment
  trigger (§5.1).

### 4.5 Expected threat (xT)
16 × 12 grid fitted on synthetic event data with our own Markov implementation (value iteration,
about 100 lines). socceraction is not used: it is no longer maintained and would be a dependency
for a few lines of maths. Cache the grid per sim version.

### 4.6 Pass metrics and speeds
- Pass distance, completion, and difficulty ∈ [0, 1] from: distance, interception risk along the
  path, pressure on passer and receiver, progression (ΔxT), line-breaking.
- Player speed from smoothed velocity. Sprint > 7.0 m/s (25.2 km/h); high-speed running > 5.5 m/s
  (19.8 km/h).
- Ball speed and shot speed from the ball trajectory.

### 4.7 Scan detector
Input: the `head_yaw` time series plus the player's and the ball's positions (needed for the ball
direction); no sim-internal state. Detect scans as excursions away from the ball direction
whose head angular velocity exceeds a threshold. The traditional 125°/s threshold is known to
over-count versus video annotation, so sweep 125–300°/s and choose by F1 against sim ground truth.
Report a precision / recall / F1 table in `eval/`, with and without the nuisance head motion
of §3.4.

## 5. Explain layer (`explain/`)

### 5.1 Moment detection
Rule-based detectors over events + analytics: goal, big chance, line-breaking pass, successful
counter-press, sprint tag (live overlay only), and *M4* a pass to the target of a blind-side alert
(§4.4). Each moment yields a `MomentCard` — the shared state for the agents: ids, time window,
involved players, key metrics, candidate lenses.
- *Implemented (M2):*
  - line-breaking pass: completed, past ≥ 2 outfield opponents;
  - decision after a scan: a reception, then the receiver's decision, with a scan ending ≤ 2 s
    before the reception;
  - sprint: > 7.0 m/s for ≥ 0.4 s, live overlay only.
  - decision under pressure (`pressed_decision`, 2026-10-20): a decision on the ball with
    pressing intensity on the carrier ≥ 0.7, the lexicon's own threshold for "under heavy
    pressure"; first in merge priority, so a pressed decision after a scan is one card with both
    tags.
  Goal, big chance and counter-press need shot outcomes and turnovers (M4).
- Detections about the same action merge into one card with several tags. Card ids look like
  `m:decision_after_scan:H8:0075`.
- **He thought vs it was.** For a decision moment, the card records P(success) of the chosen
  option on the player's belief and on the true state, at the action frame — also for the
  counterfactual run's choice. The freeze at the pass shows both ("he thought 0.05, it was 0.80").
  "It was" is the same option (same receiver) re-scored by the decision model on the true state
  (true positions and velocities, belief age 0), so its target follows the receiver's true run.
  Holding his believed target fixed would score 0 whenever he misplaced the receiver, however open
  the receiver was; the export keeps that value as `p_success_true_same_target`.
  Belief-side values are `computed` claims, counterfactual-run values are `counterfactual` claims.
- A card names its anchor events (`reception`, `last_scan`, `action`, `completion`), so claims
  anchor on `<card>.<anchor>` (§5.3).
- `content_hash()` keys the breakdown cache (§2).

### 5.2 Lens selection by counterfactual impact
For each moment, compute candidate explanations. All three deltas are in xT of the acting team.
`scan` and `space` share one value function, evaluated on the true state at the decision:
`V(option) = PC·xT(target) − (1 − PC)·xT_opp(target)` (shots keep xG).
- `scan`: drop the most informative scan — the one whose sightings move the decision-time belief
  about the eventual target most — and re-simulate (§3.7).
  Δ = V(factual choice) − V(counterfactual choice). The realised outcome difference is recorded as
  `realized_delta_xt`, but not used for selection: it is an outcome, not an expectation.
- `space`: Δ = V(chosen) − V(best alternative).
- `pressure`: expected turnover cost = pressing intensity × (xT_own + xT_opp at the ball).
  Δ = cost on the actor at the decision − cost after the action.

Pick the lens with the largest absolute delta; exact ties go `scan > space > pressure`. Record all
three in the MomentCard.

Without the scan, the player chooses one of the alternatives, so Δscan ≥ Δspace whenever the scan
changed the decision. The scan lens therefore wins exactly when the scan was decisive. An
uninformative scan scores 0, and then `space` wins (tested). On `blind_side_run`, 80 seeds
(0–39 and 100–139): `scan` is selected every time, the key scan is the most informative scan every
time, and the median margin over `space` is about 0.003 xT.
On `press_escape`, fresh held-out seeds 200–239: `pressure` is selected in 38/40 and `space` in
2/40 — the seeds where the softmax drew a poor long ball (probability 0.02) and the space lens
measures what the best pass was worth instead; `scan` never (H4's own scans change nothing,
Δscan ≈ 0). Before the decision model saw the presser's run-in (§3.5), H4 carried into the press
in 7 of seeds 100–139 and `space` won 9/40 there (now 1/40). Report:
`eval/reports/hero_lens_press_escape.md`.

### 5.3 Claims and verifier
Every factual sentence on screen comes from a `Claim`:

```json
{
  "id": "c17",
  "kind": "observed",
  "template": "{player} scanned {n_scans} times in the {window_s} seconds before receiving.",
  "slots": {"player": {"ref": "H8"}, "n_scans": {"value": 3}, "window_s": {"value": 6}},
  "predicate": {
    "op": "eq",
    "lhs": {"fn": "count_events",
            "args": {"type": "scan", "player": "H8", "from": -6.0, "to": 0.0, "anchor": "m12.reception"}},
    "rhs": 3
  },
  "evidence": ["e4512", "e4519", "e4533"]
}
```

- The verifier evaluates predicates against the event store → `supported | refuted | inconclusive`.
  Only `supported` claims render.
- Narration text uses slot placeholders; the renderer fills numbers from verified values. LLMs
  never emit digits; with a model, they do not write narration text at all (§6, anchored
  narration).
- **No placeholder survives.** A `{` or `}` left in a rendered line (a malformed or unknown
  placeholder) is a `placeholder` violation, so it never airs as literal braces.
- **Number words count as digits.** The render check rejects any number that did not come from a
  slot: ASCII digits; English number words (`one`…`twenty`, tens, `hundred`, `twice`, `thrice`,
  `dozen`, `half`, …); in `zh`, a Chinese numeral followed by a measure word (三次, 两名, 五米, …).
  Idiomatic 一 (一个, 一脚, 一眼, 一下) is allowlisted — banning 一 outright is impossible in Chinese.
- **Qualitative claims are claims too.** "Unmarked" or "under heavy pressure" carry no digit but
  are still factual. A small lexicon maps such terms to predicates, e.g. `unmarked` ⇒ nearest
  opponent > 5 m; `under heavy pressure` ⇒ pressing intensity on the carrier ≥ 0.7; `blind side`
  ⇒ outside the vision cone at the last scan (all *tunable*). A lexicon term may appear only in a
  line that references a claim verifying it. Lines without a claim reference are limited to
  connective phrases.
- Predicate ops: `eq, ne, lt, le, gt, ge, approx(tol), exists, before`.
- Functions: `count_events, metric_at, metric_delta, event_attr, distance, scans_in_window,
  observed_share, cf_delta`.
- Every verdict is written to the claims audit log (Cosmos DB) with inputs and timestamps.
- **Claim kinds, shown on screen.** Every claim is one of:
  - `observed`: an event or tracking fact (a scan count, a pass outcome);
  - `computed`: an analytics metric (pitch control, xT, belief error, pressing);
  - `counterfactual`: from re-simulating the moment with an intervention (dropping a scan). It is
    causal inside the sim only (§3.5), and the label makes that visible.
  The viewer labels each line's evidence with its kind, so facts, metrics and what-ifs never blur.
- **Banned evaluative terms.** Judgements that cannot be written as a predicate never appear in
  narration, in any persona or language — e.g. `mistake`, `error`, `dominated`, `controlled the
  game`, `certain goal`, `should have`, `lazy`, `world-class`; in `zh` 失误, 统治比赛, 必进, 本该,
  偷懒. A goal on the counter is not by itself a defensive mistake, possession is not control, and
  xG is not a certain goal. The render check rejects these like an unslotted number.
- **Render payload.** What a viewer receives for one breakdown. Each line lists its claim ids;
  each claim carries its kind, verdict, filled values, evidence event ids and a seek frame, so any
  line's evidence can be opened and the replay jumped there (§7):

```json
{"moment": "m12", "persona": "analyst", "language": "en",
 "lines": [{"id": "L2", "text": "H8 checked his shoulder once before the ball arrived.", "claims": ["c17"]}],
 "claims": [{"id": "c17", "kind": "observed", "verdict": "supported", "values": {"n_scans": 1},
             "evidence": ["scan:H8:0050"], "seek_frame": 50}]}
```

## 6. Agents (`agents/`)
Microsoft Agent Framework (Python) workflow; models via Microsoft Foundry with structured outputs;
tools exposed by our own MCP server.

| Agent | Input | Output (JSON schema) | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Director | MomentCard stream, budget | `BreakdownRequest` (moment, personas, languages, priority) | rate-limits breakdowns; picks replay slots; the audiences come from the request, the model sets only priority |
| Analyst | BreakdownRequest + MCP tools | `Findings` (lens, ranked facts as Claims) | tools: `get_moment`, `get_vision`, `get_pitch_control`, `run_counterfactual`, `get_xt`, `get_pressing` |
| Telestrator | Findings | `TelestrationPlan` | camera moves, freeze/rewind, arrows, zones, ghosts, POV segments, timecodes |
| Commentator | Findings + persona + language | anchored lines (claim id and/or connective), become `Script` lines with `speaker: "pbp"` | play-by-play voice: what happened, short and paced |
| Co-commentator | Findings + Commentator's lines + persona + language | anchored lines, become `Script` lines with `speaker: "color"` | analysis voice: why it happened, the perception story; replies to the play-by-play |
| Verifier | Script + Claims | verdicts; approve or request a rewrite | deterministic code wrapped as a workflow step |
| Voice | approved Script + TelestrationPlan | audio (Azure Speech TTS, SSML) | one voice per speaker and language; cached by content hash |

**Two voices.** The two narration agents write one interleaved `Script`, each line tagged with its
`speaker`. If either fails, a single-narrator template takes over (failure policy below).

**Delivery tiers.** Each moment gets `calm | excited | clutch` from deterministic rules on the
MomentCard (lens |Δ|, outcome, phase), never from an LLM. Voice maps the tier to an SSML speaking
style (`mstts:express-as`) where the chosen voice supports one, otherwise to prosody (rate, pitch).
Check which styles the `en` and `zh` voices support during M3.

**Failure policy.** Per-step timeout (≈ 8 s, *tunable*), one retry, then a template fallback for
that step. Refuted claim → rewrite by the agent that wrote the line (max 2) → drop the line. Every handoff is traced to
Application Insights.

*Implemented (M3, `agents/`):*
- One Agent Framework workflow; each executor handles one typed message, and the Verifier has two
  outgoing edges: back to Narration (rewrite request) or on to Voice (approved scripts).
- The Telestrator and narration read only the Analyst's findings, so a fan-out edge runs them
  concurrently. Voice places lines on the plan's clock, so it waits for both the validated plan
  and the approved scripts. That was a fan-in edge until the failure drill; it is now a join in
  Voice, so a re-run step's result is voiced again. The framework runs in supersteps, so a
  rewrite round starts after the Telestrator has finished. `trace` keeps the order Director,
  Analyst, Telestrator, narration, Verifier, Voice whichever lane finishes first.
- Models get claims as templates with slot names, never raw text to copy numbers from. The Analyst
  returns claim ids only, constrained to the supported ids by an enum in its JSON schema; a reply
  outside the enum fails validation and the Analyst's template takes over.
- **Anchored narration: the model directs, the verified templates speak.** Each narration line the
  model returns is a supported claim id, an allowed connective ("Watch this.", "注意看。"), or both;
  both are enums in the schema, so structured outputs cannot carry free text. A claim speaks its
  verified template, a connective itself (at most two per voice), and the Verifier still checks
  every line. *Why:* the first run against `gpt-4.1-mini` (M0, seed 3) produced free-text lines
  with broken grammar ("1 times"), repeated claims, invented player names, and a malformed

  with no rewrites, no drops, no invented names, and every headline claim spoken.
- Telestrator: Azure structured outputs reject the `oneOf` a discriminated union produces, so the
  model fills `PlanDraft` (a plain `anyOf` union) and `to_plan()` applies the full checks.
- *Measured (M0, `gpt-4.1-mini` GlobalStandard, northcentralus; seed 3, `--mcp`, casual and
  analyst × en and zh):* with every step in sequence, 10.5 s and 13.3 s for the whole crew

  checkpoints.

    - The run has its own key, never a normal cache key, and every drill runs the crew afresh.
    - A drill counts against the per-client rate limit and a daily cap: with a model, the daily
      model cap; offline, its own cap of 200 drills and bring-backs per UTC day. Past any of
      them: 429.
  - *Waiting.* A step that fell back after an error (a drill or a real outage) sends a workflow
    request (`AgentDown`, `request_info`): the run ends with its output aired and the request
    pending.
  - *Bring back.* `POST /api/breakdowns/<key>/bring-back` with `{"step": ...}` resumes the
    workflow from its latest checkpoint with the answer (`AgentBack`). That step runs again on
    its checkpointed input, and only the steps that read its output follow:
    - the Telestrator: Telestrator → Voice;
    - narration: narration → Verifier → Voice;
    - the Analyst or the Director: everything downstream.

    revised plan or script is voiced again. The new breakdown replaces the drill's under the same
    key. A drill can be brought back at most 5 times, so a step that keeps failing for real
    cannot be retried forever. One crew run per key at a time: a second bring-back of the same
    step waits, then answers 409 because the step no longer waits.
  - *Interrupted runs.* Every crew run in the API checkpoints at each superstep

    input and its trace entry. A breakdown interrupted mid-workflow, by a restart too, resumes
    from its last completed superstep on the next identical request. Its checkpoints are deleted
    once the result is cached. A checkpoint that no longer resumes, for example one left by older
    code, is deleted and the run starts over, so it cannot fail its key on every request.
  - *Where drills live.* A drill is one directory on the replica's disk: its record (the latest
    result, the agents still down, the bring-backs so far) and its checkpoints. Nothing is kept
    in memory or written to Cosmos DB, and only the 50 most recently used drills are kept.
  - *Limits.* Every restart drops the drills, and so does every redeploy: each green run of CI

    has to be run again. A drill whose checkpoints are missing answers 410.

  - *Tracing.* A bring-back is its own trace (`tte.bring_back`, `tte.resumed`); its step spans

## 7. Render (`web/`)
Vite + React + TypeScript + three.js via React Three Fiber (+ drei). Fictional teams and an
original visual identity.

- **Players:** rigged footballers in POV and broadcast, stylised capsule/pawn tokens with team
  colour and number on the tactical board. The tokens stay as the fallback (`?rigged=0`, and
  automatically if the model fails to load).
  - *Source:* rigged players from the Gameplay Football assets that ship
    with Google Research Football. The engine directory is under the Unlicense; we use only
    `objects/players` (segment models, `player.object` skeleton, kit template) and the
    animations, never the ad-board or stadium textures.
  - The trial is time-boxed to one day: one player with idle, walk, sprint and one pass, shown in
    the POV view. It converts to GLB with the root motion removed, the sim sets the position, the
    gait follows the sim's speed so the feet don't slide, the body follows `body_yaw`, and the
    neck joint takes `head_yaw − body_yaw`. Kits are recoloured to the fictional clubs.
  - Decided 2026-10-20: Bowen chose the rigged players by eye, so POV shows them by default and
    the broadcast camera uses them too; the tactical board keeps tokens. The trial's three
    render-side choices stand: the hips open once the head is more than 130° off the body, the
    passer turns into the pass for 0.3 s either side of the clip, and the neck stops at 80°.
- **Cameras:**
  - broadcast — high side angle, follows the ball, smoothed;
  - tactical — top-down;
  - POV — head height ≈ 1.75 m, follows `head_yaw`, FOV ≈ 95°, smoothed;
  - player-lock — for `player_fan`.
- **Overlays:**
  - sprint speed tag (live, threshold-triggered);
  - ball / shot speed burst;
  - pass line coloured by difficulty;
  - pressing rings;
  - pitch control (GLSL shader, per pixel; the tactical board's "Pitch control" toggle, off by
    default; `web/src/pitchControl/`);
  - vision cone + occlusion (shader);
  - belief ghosts (translucent "where he thought they were");
  - counterfactual ghost path;
  - *M4:* **blind-side alert** (§4.4): the target's token pulses and a thin line points to the
    observer's blind side, with how long he has gone unseen; it clears when he is seen;
  - "he thought / it was" probability tag at the action freeze (§5.1);
  - **pass options** on the tactical board: a line from the carrier to every option's target,
    width = choice probability, colour = value, the chosen one highlighted; values on hover come
    from claims. Lines go to the believed positions, with the true positions dashed; both runs
    side by side show why the decision flips. Needs the export to evaluate P(success) on the true
    state with the same function (§3.5, §5.1);
  - *M4:* **pass value surface** (xT-based, §4.2 + §4.5) as a heatmap, computed on the belief and
    on the truth; their difference is the opportunity he did not see. Not called EPV: EPV names the
    Fernández, Bornn and Cervone model trained on real tracking data (REFERENCES).
- **Breakdown player:** executes a `TelestrationPlan`; timeline with scan ticks; split view
  (POV | tactical).
- **Camera director:** the viewer picks the camera; a chip suggests which one to switch to and
  why, from deterministic rules over the live stream's events (a pass toward a player: his eyes,
  raised when he scans before the ball arrives; a defender's blind-side alert: the tactical board;
  a turnover: broadcast; a detected moment: its breakdown, or a replay with its lens's camera).
  Template reasons, no numbers, no model. An optional *Auto director* cuts by itself with a
  minimum shot length and never mid-scan ([web/src/director/](../web/src/director/)).
- **UI:** persona switch, language switch, favourite-player picker, producer console (claims with
  verdicts, audit trail, latency).
- *M4:* **"What if…?" box**: suggested questions from the hero's options plus free text; the
  answer plays as a ghost path with its verified lines (§6).
- **Evidence for viewers, not only the producer console.** Every narration line can be opened: it
  shows its claims with their kind label (observed / computed / counterfactual), verdict and
  evidence events, and seeks the replay to the evidence frame. *Implemented:* each claim with a
  number offers **Try a wrong number**: the viewer's value goes to `POST /api/verify`, the real
  Verifier re-checks the claim, and the card shows the verdict, its reason and the line struck
  through when it would not air (deep link `?tamper=<claim id>`).
- POV clips ≤ 3 s with eased yaw to avoid motion sickness.
- *Stretch:* export a breakdown as a short shareable clip in the browser (canvas + MediaRecorder,
  WebM); also usable as raw footage for the video.
- *Optional, first in the cut order (§9):* **skeleton data**, the "Skeleton" toggle on the
  broadcast camera and the tactical board, off by default ([web/src/skeleton/](../web/src/skeleton/)).
  It shows our perception signals the way limb-tracking data is shown, but draws only what the
  sim records per frame:
  - the position; the torso line from `body` (the shoulders, across the torso's facing); a head
    ray from the head yaw; and a fan from torso to head that fills in as the head–torso angle
    grows. The key reads out that angle for the player whose eyes the first-person view uses.
  - The head ray lights up while the scan detector (§4.7) marks a scan.
  - A switch picks the head yaw: **true**, or **as tracked**, with the §3.4 sensor noise (the
    series the detector runs on). The export carries the tracked yaw only as the detector's
    angular velocity, so the web app integrates that and pins the constant to the circular mean of
    true minus integrated yaw. On blind_side_run seed 3 that is within 0.38° of the sim's
    `head_yaw_observed`, every player and both runs (the noise itself reaches 7.5°).
  - A stitched match's export has no detector run: there the head yaw is the true one and the ray
    lights up during the sim's own scans, and the key says so.
  - Limbs are not sim data and are not drawn. On the broadcast camera the torso line sits at
    shoulder height and the ray at eye height; heights and the shoulder width are a drawing
    choice, not data.

### Real-footage overlay (demo video only; the one exception to synthetic data)
Bowen approved this exception himself on 2026-10-19 (CLAUDE.md hard rule 2). It is a single
segment of **≤ 10 s in the demo video**, showing the ground graphics drawn on a real camera, the
way the app draws them on the synthetic pitch: it backs the claim that the overlays would transfer
to a real broadcast.
- **Source:** the Wikimedia Commons file "Битва Корпораций - 2019-10-06 - Нова Пошта. - Мини-футбол.
  Финал." by savinkins, licensed CC BY 3.0. Checked on Commons on 2026-10-19: the license was
  reviewed by YouTubeReviewBot, and the SHA-1 matches `demo/realfeed/download.py`. It is a
  corporate-league futsal final (Атлант vs Нова Пошта).
- **Code:** `demo/realfeed/`, with OpenCV in its own dependency group.
  - It does classical computer vision only: pitch calibration, camera tracking, background
    subtraction for the players' foot points, and the turf key for the graphics.

    public app never shows or loads it.

    pitch correspondences) is committed: no frames, no player positions.

  any legible logo, sponsor or club text on kit is blurred too. Kit matters because the teams are
  companies and the video rules ban third-party trademarks.
- **Graphics only:** no numbers, captions or narration on the real footage. Every on-screen number
  in the app is a verified claim about the synthetic match, and nothing in this segment is
  verified.

  plus the attribution "savinkins, Wikimedia Commons, CC BY 3.0; blurred and overlaid by us". The
  same attribution goes in `demo/realfeed/README.md` and the repo README. CC BY requires author,
  source, license and a note of changes.

**Risks:**
1. *Rules.* The official rules say "Projects must use synthetic, football-realistic data". The
   app and all its data stay synthetic, but a judge or the Stage One viability check may still

4. *License.* CC BY 3.0 allows adaptation with attribution and a note of changes, and implies no
   endorsement. Keep the attribution on screen for the whole segment.
5. *Time.* It is near the top of the cut order (§9), after the optional skeleton view; it never
