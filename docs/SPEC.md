## 0. Pitch and judging alignment

**One-liner:** See what they saw. An AI studio that explains key moments through the player's own
perception — the scans before the ball arrived, what was visible, what he believed — and checks
every number the crew says or captions before it airs.

**Target prizes:** Grand Prize + Best Multi-Agent System (judges may reassign the category).

**Lead with perception.** Freeze frames, tactical boards, shot probabilities, paced commentary and
even deterministic number checks already appear in other broadcast-hackathon entries (REFERENCES,
"Related hackathon work"). None of them shows what the player saw or what he would have done
without one look. Open every pitch, video and README with perception and the counterfactual;
verified numbers are the backbone, not the headline.
In this competition, number checking, fan/analyst modes and multiple languages are already
standard (REFERENCES, "Competing entries"). What only we have: a perception model with a
first-person view, counterfactuals re-simulated inside the sim, and 25 Hz physical tracking with
head yaw. One entry shows "what he sees" as the passing lanes that existed; we show the lanes he
knew existed.

| Judging criterion (20% each) | How we score |
|---|---|
| Technological implementation | Physics-based synthetic tracking with a research-calibrated perception layer; numpy↔GLSL parity-tested kernels; deterministic counterfactual re-simulation; CI eval suite; Foundry + Agent Framework + MCP + Azure Speech + Container Apps + Storage static website + Cosmos DB + App Insights |
| Agentic design & innovation | AI production crew (Director, Analyst, Telestrator, Narrator, Verifier) with typed handoffs, shared moment state, MCP tools, deterministic verification, graceful fallbacks |
| Real-world impact | Production path: the same schema can be fed by stadium skeletal tracking or broadcast-video models; machine-readable, timecoded overlay cues; audit log of every verified claim |
| UX & presentation | First-person replay + tactical camera + live overlays; persona and language switching; a 2-minute video built around one hero moment |
| Category adherence | Distinct agent roles, shared state, handoffs, failure recovery, an end-to-end output a single agent can't produce as well |

## 1. Audiences (personas)

| Persona | Gets | Tone |
|---|---|---|
| `casual` | First-person replay + a one-sentence story | plain language, minimal numbers |
| `analyst` | Scan rate vs research benchmarks, time since last scan at reception, observed-space share, belief error, counterfactual delta, pitch-control / pressing overlays | dense, technical |
| `player_fan` | Camera locked on the chosen player all match; his scans before each reception; his best decisions | personal |
| `coach` (stretch) | "Scanned vs didn't scan" side-by-side + one coaching cue | instructional |
| `audio_description` | Blind and low-vision viewers: the moment told in the order it happened — where he got the ball, who he could not see, which way he looked, whom the look showed him, what he did — sound on by default, large-print captions | calm, slower voice at any tier; no xT or probabilities |

All personas render the **same verified fact set**. Persona changes presentation, never facts:
which verified claims a script must cite and which it may not is fixed per persona in code (§6,
"Persona rules").
Languages: `en` and `zh` for the MVP; `es` is a stretch goal.

**Latency model.** Live overlays come from deterministic analytics only (target p95 < 100 ms
server-side). AI breakdowns run asynchronously once a moment is detected and are shown in the
replay window (target p95 < 12 s), the way broadcast replays follow a goal. Live play never waits
on an LLM.

**The cold load** (a judge who opens the page and only watches; web/src/App.tsx,
[web/src/autoplay/](../web/src/autoplay/)):
- The lineup card opens first. Behind it the picture rests on the stream's first frame. The hero
  moment's stream starts only when the card closes, by itself after 5 s or on a click: its key
  scan comes in the first seconds and must not play under the card. A stitched match's stream
  plays behind the card; its first possession is open play.
- When the stream detects the hero moment, the show plays at once; it does not wait for the
  breakdown:
  1. the look, replayed (slowed around the scan);
  2. the same seconds re-simulated without it, the other run's path as a ghost on the board.
- Each part shows its verified line from the breakdown as soon as the breakdown is there ("Without
  that look, H8 would have passed to H4.").
- Then the breakdown plays.
- Any viewer action ends the show.
- Measured: the run without the look and its line are on screen 16–17 s after opening the page
  (local API, headless Chrome).

*Implemented (`api/`, M3):* FastAPI.
- `WS /ws/matches/<id>/live?speed=1` replays the factual run at 25 Hz with `frame`, `event`,
  `moment` and `end` messages (`full_time: true` when it reached the last frame); clients can
  pause, resume, seek, change speed or stop.
- REST: `/api/matches`, `/api/matches/<id>` (+ `/export`, `/moments`, `/audit`),
  `POST /api/breakdowns` (`fail` for the failure drill, §6),
  `POST /api/breakdowns/<key>/bring-back`, `POST /api/whatif`, `POST /api/tts` (503 until Speech
  is configured), `POST /api/verify` (one claim re-checked with one numeric slot changed; not
  audited, rate-limited per client, no model call), `POST /api/recaps` and
  `GET /api/recaps/<key>/cues` (the end-of-match recap and its overlay cue sheet, §6),
  `GET /api/breakdowns/<key>/trace` and `GET /api/recaps/<key>/trace` (the crew trace, §6).
- Every breakdown, drill and recap answer says where it came from (`provenance`): `live` (the
  crew ran for this request), `cached` (a crew run from `ran_at`, UTC), or `template` (no model
  call, with the reason: `rate_limited`, `capped` or `model_unavailable`). The web shows it as a
  chip on the moment card, the narration, the recap and the crew trace, exactly as given: it is
  never worked out from `cached` or `source`, so a cached or template answer never reads as a
  fresh crew run. An answer from an older service without it makes no claim about who wrote it.
- The service is public with no login (§8), so it is bounded: `POST /api/tts` takes a voice cue
  key from a breakdown it served, never SSML, and is rate-limited per client; the live stream
  allows a few streams per client and a session length, and closes on a malformed control
  message; request bodies are capped at 16 KiB; personas and languages are deduplicated; responses
  carry security headers, and a 500 carries no traceback.
- Measured locally: 25.0 fps with a p95 frame gap of 41 ms; a cached breakdown is served in about
  1 ms.
- The cache is one JSON file per key under `TTE_CACHE_DIR`; Cosmos DB can replace it (§8).

**Data flow.**
- The sim runs offline and writes match files. `api/` replays a match at 1× wall-clock as the live
  ingest stream (frames + events over WebSocket), so Ingest really happens "as events happen"
  (rules: *Ingest synthetic match events as they happen*) while the sim stays deterministic and cheap.
- Moment detection runs on the live stream. Counterfactuals are re-simulated from snapshots on
  demand, and precomputed for the demo match.
- **Breakdown cache.** Every agent output is cached under a content hash of
  (MomentCard, persona, language, prompt version, model deployment). The public demo serves cached
  breakdowns by default; live regeneration is rate-limited per IP. The demo has no login and must
  stay up until 2026-11-10, so it must survive exhausted model quota or credit.

## 3. Synthetic match engine (`sim/`)

### 3.1 State and schema
- **A match is 15–25 scenario-driven possessions (§3.6) stitched together with restarts**
  (kick-off, throw-in, goal kick, corner): 10–15 min of play in total, not 90 min of free play.
  Making 22 free agents look like football is the biggest time sink, and hero moments must be
  controllable anyway. Rates in the realism report are normalised per 90 min.
  *Implemented (2026-10-21, `sim/match.py`):* `match-1` has 19 possessions: 15 open-play
  passages and four heroes. On this Mac it lasts 808.3 s (13:28) and ends 1–0. The heroes start
  at 0:30 (`blind_side_run` seed 3, hero moment at 0:33), 5:07 (`press_escape` seed 0), 7:58 (the
  shot hero `far_post_finish` seed 11, who scores at the far post) and 9:47 (the set-piece hero
  `corner_blind_side` seed 0, at 1–0).
  - *Seeds.* Hero possessions keep their demo seeds. An open-play seed is keyed by (match seed,
    n), where n counts open-play passages only, so a hero inserted later changes no open-play
    seed. Seeds start at 1,000,000 and are never design or eval seeds.
  - *Caps.* An open-play passage plays until the ball goes out or its cap. The caps are fixed in
    the plan: 30 s for the kick-off passage, so the first hero comes early, then 40–70 s, varied
    for rhythm. The match is therefore at most 13:28 on any platform (780 s of caps and 27.8 s of
    heroes). With the team shape (§3.2) a pass nobody reaches is fetched, and `possession_play`
    put no ball out in 40 runs of 45 s (tuning seeds 0–39), so every passage reaches its cap: on
    this Mac none ends in a throw-in, and match-1 runs its full 13:28. (Before the team shape 2 of
    15 did, 4 before players were kept on the pitch, and where the ball went out depended on the
    platform, §3.7: the live service, Linux x86, ran the 18-possession version for 13:06 on
    2026-10-21 against 11:31 on this Mac.)
  - *Not kept: picking passages by how they end (tried 2026-10-21).* Each passage was the first
    keyed candidate that ended out of play after 25–60 s, with the attempts pinned per match
    seed, so every stitch after open play was a stoppage. It is not portable. The sim is byte
    for byte only within one platform (§3.7), and on CI's Linux x86 runner the second passage
    pinned on this Mac no longer ended out of play, so the service failed to start. Searching
    at startup instead would simulate about 4,800 s of play, minutes on 0.5 vCPU.
  - *Restarts.* A restart is how a possession begins. The first kicks off, and so does one after
    a goal. A scenario that opens with a set piece begins with it (`corner_blind_side`: a
    corner). Otherwise play restarts the way the previous possession stopped: a throw-in, goal
    kick or corner when the whole ball went out, an indirect free kick after an offside call
    (§3.9), a `cut` when it ended in play. The team the Laws award the restart to starts the
    next open-play passage (2026-10-22, `sim.match.awarded_team`): the side that conceded kicks off
    (Law 8.1), a throw-in goes to the opponents of the last player to touch the ball (15.1), a
    goal kick to the defenders and a corner to the attackers (16.1, 17.1), the indirect free kick
    after an offside call to the defenders (11.4). Open play with the away team on the first ball
    is its own scenario, `possession_play_away` (`PossessionPlay.start_team`), so every possession
    still re-simulates from its scenario and seed. After a cut no law awards the ball, and the
    home team starts. On this Mac:
    2 kick-offs (the start, and after the goal), 1 corner, 16 cuts. The next scenario sets up its own situation: the
    stitch is an edit, not a simulated restart.
  - *Score.* Goals are `goal` events, from scenarios with shots on (§3.3): the score is counted,
    never set. Only `far_post_finish` shoots in match-1. Seed 11 scores at the far post with the
    look; without it, the counterfactual goes near post and A1 saves it (§3.6).
  - *match-2* (`sim.match.MATCH_2`, 2026-10-21): three of match-1's heroes on other design seeds,
    in another order: `press_escape` 1 from 0:25, `corner_blind_side` 2 from 4:01 and
    `blind_side_run` 11 from 8:37. It has 17 possessions and runs every cap (§3.2), 743.8 s
    (12:24) on this Mac, and no shooting hero, so it ends 0–0.
    Each new seed passed the checks its demo seed passes: the hero moment and its lens are found,
    the scenario's intervention changes the outcome (the decision; whether the man is marked as
    the ball arrives; the possession after a forced carry), every claim is supported, and an
    offline breakdown for every persona and language has no render violation. Of the seeds
    screened, only `press_escape` 8 failed: its breakdown crashed in `runner_received` on an
    intercepted pass (fixed in #89). The other seeds were picked for a typical size and a different
    story: wide to H7 without the look; out to the full-back H3 under the press; 3.0 m with the
    look and 8.1 m without, at the corner. The API serves it as `match-2` (`TTE_MATCHES=…,match:2`),
    listed in the match picker; the default stays where it was.
  - Each possession stays its own `MatchRun`: moments, lenses, counterfactuals and breakdowns run
    per possession and are lifted to match time (`explain/match.py`). A possession's hero moment
    is `explain.moments.hero_card`: the hero's decision, or for the corner hero, who has no ball
    decision, the marker's look (`marker_scan`, §5.1). The API serves the match as `match-1`
    (`TTE_MATCHES=…,match:1`), and every hero possession of a served match on its own as well,
    listed or not: the web app opens a hero moment's breakdown from that export. The export is
    the hero export's run schema over the whole clock plus a `match` block: 11.6 MB of JSON,
    4.0 MB gzipped, compressed once when the match loads.
- Pitch 105 × 68 m, origin at the centre spot; the home team attacks +x in the first half. 25 Hz.
- Per frame, per player: `id, team, role, x, y, vx, vy, body_yaw, head_yaw` (world frame), `has_ball`.
- Ball: `x, y, z, vx, vy, vz, owner`.
- Events: `pass, reception, carry, shot, tackle, interception, pressure, possession_change, out,
  goal, offside, post, scan, head_turn` (`offside` and `post`: §3.9) with `id, t, frame, player, team, attrs` (e.g. pass start/end, speed,
  outcome, difficulty). Ids are `<type>:<player>:<frame>`, stable across factual and
  counterfactual runs.
- `scan` events are **ground truth** from the sim. The analytics scan detector must recover them
  from tracking data only — `head_yaw` plus player and ball positions — with no access to sim
  scan events or other sim-internal state (§4.7).
- Output: JSONL frames + JSON events. Optional kloppy-compatible export (stretch).
- *Downloadable dataset (2026-10-21, `sim/dataset.py`):* stitched matches on fresh seeds as
  events (CSV/Parquet), 25 Hz tracking (Parquet), metadata (JSON) and per-reception perception,
  with a datasheet: [DATASET.md](DATASET.md).

### 3.2 Movement
- **Team shape** (2026-10-22, `sim/team_shape.py`, behind `Scenario.team_shape`; on for
  `possession_play`, the open play of every stitched match). Each off-ball player's target is his
  slot in his team's block: a 4-3-3 at home and a 4-2-3-1 away with the ball, a 4-1-4-1 and a
  4-4-1-1 without it (Shaw & Glickman measure formations in and out of possession apart).
  - The team on the ball is in its attacking shape, the other in its defending shape; the phase
    switches when possession does, and the players then move between the two shapes under the
    usual limits, so a transition is that movement, not a third template.
  - The back line is one line of four. Without the ball its height follows the regression
    Castellano & Álvarez (2013) fit on La Liga matches, 6.30 m + 0.46 × the ball's distance from
    the goal defended; with it, it stays a set distance behind the ball within a deepest and a
    highest line (no paper prints a relation for it). The front line never goes beyond the
    opponents' back line unless the ball does. The block is shorter and narrower without the ball, slides toward
    the ball's side, and follows the ball smoothed over a second rather than the ball itself.
  - Full-backs push on in possession; the far-side wide player tucks in without it; the keeper
    is not in the block: he takes his spot (below) and no longer presses or chases passing lanes.
  - A player making for his slot jogs (3 m/s) and runs harder the further out of position he is,
    at full speed from 25 m: with the block on the instant ball and full speed to every slot,
    15% of the time was above 5.5 m/s (5% now, 3% before the shape).
  - The block's sizes and the in-possession line were fitted by hand on tuning seeds 0–39 to the
    means Castellano et al. (2013) print in and out of possession and Forcher et al. (2024) print
    for settled defence (docs/REFERENCES.md, Team shape). On held-out seeds 100–199
    ([realism.md](../eval/reports/realism.md), Team shape) those eight rows are inside. Six are
    outside: Errekagorri et al.'s (2024) per-match means over 5,518 LaLiga team-matches, both
    phases together (the sim's team is 34.0 m long against 36.9 ± 1.7, 39.2 m wide against
    43.4 ± 2.1, its deepest player 32.6 m out against 37.3 ± 3.0, its keeper 21.9 m from the
    nearest team-mate against 25.0 ± 1.7), which were not fitted to: Castellano et al.'s one
    team is narrower, shorter and deeper than that league, and `possession_play` keeps the ball
    nearer its own goal than a match does; and the line's regression on the ball, which comes
    out at 8.94 m + 0.405 × against the paper's 6.30 + 0.46 × (r 0.75 against 0.86). Without the
    shape 4 of the 14 are inside, a team is longer out of possession than in it, and the
    regression's slope is 0.226. Slot depths, the tuck, the push and the speeds are style knobs.
  - The hero scenarios do not set the flag, so their runs, gates and reports are unchanged, and
    neither does `open_play`: the xT grid every moment's ΔxT reads is fitted on it (§4.5). A
    hero scenario's off-ball players could join once its gate is re-run on held-out seeds.
- **Goalkeeper positioning** (2026-10-22, `sim/keeper.py`; every scenario). Where a keeper stands
  when no script moves him:
  - On the bisector of the angle the ball makes with his two posts, so he leaves equal room
    inside each post (angle play).
  - Off his goal line by his defensive line (his deepest outfield player; with the team shape, the
    back line): 20.4 m behind it, never nearer his goal line than 4.8 m. FIFA's open-play tracking
    printed both: keepers at the 2022 World Cup averaged 13.1 m off their line and 20.4 m behind
    their defensive line, and at the 2021 Club World Cup one keeper behind a 17.4 m line averaged
    4.8 m (docs/REFERENCES.md, Goalkeeper positioning). Holding the gap down to the floor is a
    style knob: no source prints the depth against the line's or the ball's, and the rule
    reproduces both anchors.
  - Inside his penalty area, 1 m from its edges (he handles only there, Law 12.1), and at least
    1 m nearer his goal line than the ball.
  - He leaves his spot for a pass only where he would claim it inside his own area; before, a
    keeper in a scenario with neither the team shape nor shots chased every pass across the
    pitch. Scripts still move keepers (`keeper_switch`, `screened_shot`, `far_post_finish`'s
    set) and the shot model's set position overrides the spot (§3.3).
  - Measured on held-out seeds (45 s runs): in `possession_play` 100–119 keepers average 11.2 m
    off their line and 21.0 m behind their defensive line (12.8 and 19.5 m before), inside their
    area on every frame; in `blind_side_run` 100–109 on 97.8% of frames (90.8% before; the rest is
    one back-pass he meets with his feet 18.8 m out). Tests: `tests/test_keeper.py`.
  - Every hero gate is unchanged on its held-out seeds. In `open_play` keepers no longer chase
    passes, so the xT grid was refitted (§4.5). Seed 3 moved on screen (Bowen approved,
    2026-10-22): the look is worth 0.16 xT (0.17 before), and H8 thought the pass to H9 had a 0.65
    chance against 0.83 true (0.67 and 0.85 before), with A1 and A3 standing differently.
- Steering toward targets with limits — *tunable*: max speed by role 8.0–9.5 m/s, max accel
  ≈ 3.5 m/s², max decel ≈ 5 m/s², turn-rate limit.
- Pitch bounds (2026-10-21, `sim/engine.py`, `sim/movement.py`): every movement target and
  every setup position is clamped to the pitch, and a player brakes for the line ahead (the
  exact discrete stopping envelope at the max decel, sharing the frame's accel/decel limit with
  the rest of his step), so keepers stay at or in front of their goal line and nobody crosses a
  touchline (`tests/test_on_pitch.py`: every frame, 1e-6 m tolerance; the measured worst is 0).
  Clamping targets alone was not enough: a player who turns along a line keeps drifting over it.
  Before, the formation shift and a free ball's predicted path, which runs on past the lines, put
  the away keeper up to 8 m behind his goal line in `blind_side_run` (1.7 m on the demo seed) and
  full-backs and wingers past the touchline in open play. No restart is simulated (§3.1), so no
  player ever needs to stand outside. Fewer balls go out as a result: 1.0 per stitched match
  instead of 2.4 ([match_stats](../eval/reports/match_stats.md)).
- Separation steering to avoid overlaps (Python-RVO2 optional, see REFERENCES).
- Pressing: the nearest 1–2 defenders converge on the ball carrier when a team pressing trigger fires.
- Marking (`possession_play`): every other outfield defender takes an attacker in his zone and stays
  goal-side of him, 3–6 m off or, in half of his 10 s spells (keyed draws), tight at 1–2 m. So a
  receiver under tight pressure has usually been marked for a while, and the scan slowdown under
  pressure (§3.4) can show in the realism report. A passer judges a teammate's space without the
  opponents beyond him along the pass, such as a goal-side marker: a ball to his feet gets there first.
  With the team shape the marking is zonal: a defender picks up an attacker within 12 m of his slot
  and lets him go when following him would take him more than 15 m from it, so the block keeps its
  shape (man to man anywhere in a 20 m zone, it was 47 m wide without the ball). A pass is played
  ahead of a receiver on the move, and a pass its receiver cannot reach is chased by a team-mate.

### 3.3 Ball physics
- Flight: gravity + quadratic drag — *tunable*: m = 0.43 kg, r = 0.11 m, ρ = 1.2 kg/m³, C_d ≈ 0.25.
  Magnus optional.
- Ground: rolling friction; bounce with restitution ≈ 0.5 (*tunable*).
- Passes and shots solve launch speed/angle to reach the target (ground vs lofted). Shot speeds
  roughly 80–130 km/h by context.
- *M1:* ground passes only, in closed form (rolling resistance 0.6 m/s² + quadratic drag), used
  both for frame stepping and for the decision model's predictions. Longer passes are played to
  arrive faster (arrival speed 0.35 × distance, clipped to 6–12 m/s). Lofted passes, bounce, Magnus
  and shot outcomes are M4.
- *Shots and keepers (Module A of the hero expansion pack, 2026-10-21; `sim/shots.py`).* Only in
  scenarios with `Scenario.shots`. With the flag off nothing changes.
  - The committed `blind_side_run` seed 3 export is byte-identical
    (`tests/test_committed_exports.py`, the only export it pins).
  - The `hero_flip` (blind_side_run), `hero_lens` (press_escape) and `hero_marking` reports
    regenerate with an empty diff.
  - Every other eval report is re-run on the merge by the core review desk.
  - Sources, and how far each was read: `docs/REFERENCES.md`, "Shots and goalkeepers".
  - **The shot.** Low and driven, on the ground, using the rolling physics above.
    - Speed is U(24, 30) m/s per shot (a keyed draw), inside the 18–35 m/s of maximal instep kicks
      (Kellis & Katis 2007).
    - It is aimed 0.5 m inside the near or far post, with a lateral aim error of N(0, 2.5°).
      That value is *tunable*: Hunter et al. (2018) give the shape (error variance grows with
      speed), not our number.
    - The decision model offers `shot:near` and `shot:far` instead of `shot`. Each is valued on
      the shooter's belief as P(on target) × (1 − E[P_save]), the expectation taken over where he
      believes the keeper is, with the belief's position uncertainty.
  - **The keeper.**
    - He stands on the bisector of the ball's angle to his posts, 1.5 m off his line. This is a
      convention, not a cited model.
    - He reacts from his first sight of the ball, the first frame with visibility ≥ 0.5, using the
      same field of view and occlusion as every player (§3.4).
    - During a shot he holds his set position until then. Behind the flag, keepers do not chase
      passing lanes.
  - **The save model** is Bransen, Janssen & Davis (2025), eq. 2, fitted on real penalties with
    the keeper in the correct corner. It is an arXiv preprint.
    - P_save = ρ inside r − µ, falling linearly to 0 at r + µ, with ρ = 0.7 and µ = 0.7 m.
    - The reach is r(T) = clip(2.8 + v_dive · (T − 0.55 s), 0.3, 3.1) m. T runs from his first
      sight of the ball to its reaching his line. 2.8 and 3.1 m are the fitted late and early dive
      ranges; 0.55 s is the 500–600 ms penalty flight time they cite.
    - v_dive has no verified source. Ibrahim et al. (2019) print the legs' contributions to the
      centre of mass's velocity, not a take-off speed, and a literature check found no primary
      number. So every keeper gate is re-run at v_dive = 2.5 and 4.0 m/s.
  - **Events.** `shot` (placement, speed, error, crossing points, first sight, reach, P_save,
    outcome), `dive`, `save`, `goal`. Shot outcomes are goal, saved, wide or blocked.
    - A goal is worth 1.0 in possession value.
    - A stitched match restarts with a kick-off after a goal.

### 3.4 Perception (the hero feature)
- **Head yaw dynamics.** Baseline gaze tracks the ball. A **scan** is a rapid turn away from the
  ball and back. Most scans are short: >90% last ≤ 0.66 s in eye-tracking work (see REFERENCES).
- **Scan scheduling.** Stochastic process with rate by role and context, plus a per-player trait
  multiplier. Calibration targets from research:
  - ≈ 3 scans in the 10 s before receiving, on average;
  - > 0.5 scans/s counts as very high (elite central midfielders);
  - central midfielders and centre-backs scan most, forwards least;
  - scanning drops under tight pressure.

  *Calibrated values* (`eval/scan_calibration.py`, tuning seeds 0–99; held-out check in
  `eval/reports/realism.md`): each scan turns at a peak speed drawn from 404 ± 51°/s (Kokubu et
  al. 2026); the dwell is lognormal, so 90% of scans keep the ball out of an 82° frame ≤ 0.66 s
  and a few hold the look for up to ~3 s (Aksum et al. 2021); the rate scales with the closest
  opponent's distance, from 0.61 at ≤ 1 m to 1 at ≥ 10 m (Jordet et al. 2020); role rates are
  fitted to ≈ 3 scans in the 10 s before receiving and to Jordet et al.'s role effect sizes. The
  per-player trait multiplier is not in yet.
- **Non-scan head motion (nuisance).** So the scan detector is not graded on a signal we made
  clean ourselves, the sim also produces head motion that is not a scan: fast ball tracking during
  passes, sensor noise (σ ≈ 2°, *tunable*), partial/aborted scans and slow drift. Partial turns
  are `head_turn` events (`attrs.partial = true`), never `scan` events: they are not scans.
- **Field of view** (after Bekkers 2026, *Wide Open Gazes*): base 120° binocular cone around
  `head_yaw`; visibility decays with angular offset and distance (Gaussian); the cone narrows as
  running speed rises.
- **Occlusion.** Other players block angular sectors behind them (soft edges).
- **Belief state (mental map).** For every other player: last-seen position, velocity and time.
  Belief = extrapolation from last sighting; uncertainty grows with time since seen. Updated
  whenever that player falls inside the probabilistic visible region.
  *M1 values:* a player counts as seen when V ≥ 0.5; beliefs are extrapolated at most 1 s;
  position uncertainty = 0.3 m + 1.0 m per second of age (all *tunable*).

### 3.5 Decision model
- The ball carrier evaluates candidate actions (pass to each teammate, carry, shot) **on its
  belief state**, never on ground truth.
- Pass value = P(completion | belief) × ΔxT(target). P comes from interception risk along the ball
  path (defender time-to-intercept vs ball arrival time) and receiver pitch control at the target.
  Implemented as expected threat after the action, P·xT(target) − (1 − P)·xT_opp(target): the
  current xT is the same for every option and drops out of the softmax. P also includes the chance
  that the receiver gets there before the ball has rolled on (0.5 s slack).
- **An opponent can also run into the ball before he reacts** (2026-10-20). TTI (§4.1) has him keep
  his velocity for the reaction time, and its closed form only counts arrivals after it, so a
  presser sprinting at the carrier, or an opponent standing on the passing lane, looked as if the
  ball got past him. Under the same motion model, the decision model also takes the closest
  approach of his reaction-time run to the ball, at the same moment, from t = 0 along the ball's
  path (pass) or the carry (5 m in 1 s); within the sim's control radius (1 m, the radius its
  control and tackle rules use) he wins it. P = logistic of (radius − closest approach) with the
  spread of the belief's position uncertainty (0.3 m + 1 m per second of belief age); no new
  tuned parameter. Per opponent the larger of the two chances counts. The TTI kernel itself
  (pitch control, pressing, shaders) is unchanged. Test: `tests/test_decision_run_in.py`.
- A carry is scored along its path like a pass (TTI at each sample of the path, from 0.08 s to
  1 s). While the carry is no faster than v_max (5 m in 1 s at v_max 5 m/s, as now) the TTI margin
  never falls along it, so the endpoint is its worst point and the result is the same as before.
  *Limit:* after the reaction, TTI is to a point of the path, not to within the 1 m control
  radius, for passes, carries and pitch control alike. An opponent who would step to within 1 m
  of the ball just after his reaction, but not onto its path in time, is under-counted, e.g. one
  standing 1.2 m beside a 5 m carry, 3 m along it, gets P(keep) 0.64, while the sim's tackle rule
  takes the ball from anyone who gets within 1 m of it.
- Each teammate is evaluated at several targets along his believed run (lead 0–2 s); the best one
  is his option. Decisions value options with a smooth xT prior, not the fitted grid (§4.5): the
  grid is fitted on play this model drives, so valuing options with it would change its own
  training data. Analytics and explain read the fitted grid.
- Softmax with a per-player temperature. Receiver body orientation affects completion (facing play
  vs back to goal) — not yet in M1.
- **Sampling is Gumbel-max** with keyed noise per (player, frame, option): argmax(value/T + g) is
  exactly a softmax draw, and in a counterfactual an option whose value did not change keeps its
  noise (a Gumbel-max structural causal model, Oberst & Sontag 2019). A single uniform over a
  cumulative distribution would make the flip depend on option order.
- Consequence: scanning changes information, which changes decisions. This is what makes the
  explanation causal *inside the sim*. Outside the sim (real data) we only claim association.

### 3.6 Scenario templates (hero moments)
Parameterised templates that set up recognisable situations inside normal play: counter-attack
after a turnover, third-man run, switch of play, high-press trap, through-ball behind a high line.
Randomised parameters; the same physics and decision code. Documented in the README as
scenario-driven generation.

**Hero design rule.** In a scan-lens hero scenario, the free option (e.g. the runner) is visible to
the decision-maker only during the key scan, and the last sighting before it shows him marked or in
a low-value position. `drop_scan` then flips the decision because of how the scene is built, not by
chance.

### 3.7 Determinism and counterfactuals
- One match seed. **Randomness is keyed, not sequential:** every draw comes from a generator keyed
  by `(match_seed, entity_id, purpose)` that draws a fixed number of values per frame whatever
  happens in the sim (or, equivalently, a generator keyed by frame as well). A single shared
  stream is not allowed: an intervention such as `drop_scan` would change how many values are
  drawn, shift every later draw, and mix noise into the counterfactual delta. Keyed draws give
  common random numbers — factual and counterfactual runs differ only through the intervention.
- **Byte-identical within one environment.** Same seed + same platform + same numpy version ⇒
  byte-identical output. Serialised output is quantised (positions and velocities to 1 mm and
  1 mm/s, angles to 1e-4 rad). Cross-platform identity is not promised: numpy's SIMD
  transcendental functions can differ in the last bits between ARM and x86, and a multi-agent sim
  amplifies that. CI compares runs inside CI, never against hashes made on a laptop.
- `snapshot(t)` / `resume(snapshot, overrides)` re-simulate from t with overrides such as
  `drop_scan(scan_id)`, `freeze_belief(player, at=t')`, `move_player(...)`,
  `drop_step(step_id)` (a scripted step not taken; `screened_shot`, §3.6), and
  `force_action(player, frame, action)` (*M4*): at that decision the player takes `action` — any
  option he evaluated, e.g. `pass:H4` — instead of sampling; everything else re-simulates as
  usual. It answers "what if he had passed to H4?" with a re-simulation, not a statistic.
- **A look the viewer chooses** (2026-10-21, for Take his eyes, §7): `add_scan(player, frame,
  offset)` (`sim.overrides.AddScan`) starts one scan at that frame, `offset` from the ball
  (counter-clockwise positive). It takes the path of the scenario's forced scans, so it is as
  physical as any scan: the amplitude must lie in `SimParams.scan_amplitude` (70–150°), the head
  turns at the scan's keyed peak speed (404 ± 51°/s, §3.4), and the dwell is the median of
  `SimParams.scan_dwell_s`. A turn outside that range is refused. `no_scans(player, start, end)`
  (`NoScans`) stops his own spontaneous and partial scans in a window.
  - `look_counterfactual` gives the hero **one look**, from the pass to him until his decision:
    his own scans in that window (the key scan, spontaneous and partial ones) do not happen, and
    the viewer's look is his only one. Looks added on top of his own would collide with them: a
    forced scan waits for one under way, so an extra look before the key scan cancels it, and in
    `press_escape` seed 0 his own looks fill the window so that additive looks could start only
    in its first 0.9 s.
  - With no look, the scan hero's run is the run without his key scan byte for byte.
  - A look changes only his head and what he believes until he decides: positions, the ball,
    every other player's head and events, and every random draw are unchanged
    (`tests/test_look.py`; `sim.export.export_looks` refuses a look that breaks this).
  - Ids: `look:H8:0050:-150` (a look at frame 50, 150° clockwise of the ball: over his right
    shoulder as he faces it) and `look:H8:none`; `what_if_from_id` and the Verifier re-simulate
    them like `force:` ids.
- A counterfactual result = alternative decision + outcome + ΔxT / Δ shot probability.
- **Seed registry.** Scenario seeds and match seeds are separate inputs, but no range below is
  reused for another purpose.

  | Seeds | What | Where |
  |---|---|---|
  | 0–1999 | scenario seeds: each scenario's design seeds and its held-out eval seeds | §3.6, §4, `eval/` reports |
  | 0, 3, 11 | demo hero seeds (`press_escape`/`corner_blind_side` 0, `blind_side_run` 3, `far_post_finish` 11) | `sim/match.py` |
  | 1, 2 | match seeds of the served matches | `sim/match.py`, `api/` |
  | 100–119 | held-out match seeds | `eval/match_stats.py` |
  | 5001–5100 | match seeds of the downloadable dataset; v1 uses 5001–5010 | `sim/dataset.py`, [DATASET.md](DATASET.md) |
  | ≥ 1,000,000 | scenario seeds keyed off a match seed: open-play passages (`open_play`), the dataset's heroes (`dataset_hero`) | `sim/match.py`, `sim/dataset.py` |

### 3.9 Laws of the Game (2026-10-22)
The sim plays by the IFAB Laws of the Game as hard rules: it never breaks a law without the
referee calling it. Bowen asked for it on 2026-10-22 ("把足球裁判员手册写进sim写死"), after he saw that
the flagship pass H8 → H9 of `blind_side_run` was offside. The audit found it offside on every
seed. The source is the IFAB *Laws of the Game 2026/27* on theifab.com, read law by law on
2026-10-22 ([REFERENCES.md](REFERENCES.md#the-laws-of-the-game-spec-39)): where the Laws and
intuition differ, the Laws win. Each rule is cited by law and section ("IFAB Laws of the Game,
Law 11.2") and restated in our own words; no text, diagram or figure of the Laws is in the repo.

- **Where.**
  - `sim/laws.py`: constants and pure checks over a frame or an event, with no engine imports.
  - `sim/referee.py`: the referee in the frame loop, called by `sim/engine.py`.
  - `eval/laws.py`: the audit of every scenario on design and held-out seeds, each hero's
    intervention, and both served matches, written to
    [eval/reports/laws.md](../eval/reports/laws.md).
  - Tests: [tests/test_laws_checks.py](../tests/test_laws_checks.py) (hand-built snapshots,
    every offside edge case), [tests/test_laws_audit.py](../tests/test_laws_audit.py) and
    [tests/test_laws.py](../tests/test_laws.py) (the invariant sweep: no unpunished breach, no
    wrong or early call).
  - The web's pitch is checked against `sim/laws.py` by `tests/test_pitch_geometry.py`.
- **Body model.** Players are points: the centre of the body on the ground. For offside the Laws
  count head, body and feet, never the hands or arms (Law 11.1), and compare the attacker's part nearest the goal line
  with the second-last opponent's. Every player has the same body, so its depth cancels and the
  comparison is point against point; the ball counts by its centre. "Level" means within 1 mm,
  the resolution positions are written at (§3.7).

| Law | What the sim does |
|---|---|
| 1, the field | 105 × 68 m, inside the international range (1.4). Lines 12 cm, each belonging to the area it bounds (1.2). Goal area 5.5 m (1.5); penalty area 16.5 m, penalty mark 11 m, penalty arc 9.15 m (1.6); centre circle 9.15 m (1.2); corner arc 1 m (1.7). Goal 7.32 m between the inner edges of the posts, 2.44 m to the bar's lower edge; round posts on the goal line, their backs on its outer edge (1.10 allows round ones). `sim.config`, `sim.shots`, `analytics.xt` and the web app use these numbers (tests). |
| 2, the ball | Radius 0.11 m, inside the 68–70 cm circumference (0.108–0.111 m), and 0.43 kg, inside 410–450 g (2.1). |
| 3, the players | Eleven a side, one goalkeeper each, in every scenario (3.1; test). |
| 8, kick-off | Not simulated: a match's kick-offs are stitches (§3.1). `laws.restart_breaches` checks one (8.1). |
| 9, ball in and out of play | Out once the whole ball is over a goal line or a touchline, carried or rolling, on the first frame it has wholly crossed (9.1). A ball back off a post stays in play (9.2). Until 2026-10-22 it was out when its centre crossed, and a carried ball never went out. |
| 10, a goal | Any ball that wholly crossed the goal line between the posts and under the bar, whoever touched it last, and only if the scoring team committed no offence first (10.1). An own goal counts for the attacking team (`own_goal`). A ball that meets a post bounces off it (`sim.ball.post_contact`, restitution 0.5, tunable and uncited). Until then a ball through the post counted as a goal or as wide, and a pass that rolled in was called out. |
| 11, offside | At the first contact of a team-mate's pass or shot the referee notes who is in an offside position: in the opponents' half (the halfway line excluded), nearer their goal line than the ball and the second-last opponent; level is onside (11.1). Off-field players count as on the line (11.4). The offence needs involvement in active play (11.2): he plays or touches the ball, including after it came back off a post (gaining an advantage: the watch survives the rebound); he challenges an opponent for it (within the 1 m control radius); or he clearly blocks the keeper's line of vision at a shot. A clear attempt to play a close ball and an obvious action are judgement calls the sim has no data for. No offence straight from a goal kick, throw-in or corner (11.3). Play stops with an `offside` event and an indirect free kick to the defenders where he became involved (11.4); the pass's outcome is `offside` and the stitched match restarts with `free_kick`. |
| 11, players who know it | Attackers without the ball hold HOLD_MARGIN (1 m) short of the line while their team has the ball, except the intended receiver of a pass in flight (`Referee.hold_the_line`). A scripted run is timed by its scenario. The decision model multiplies a pass's P(success) by 1 − P(offside) on the carrier's belief, with the belief's position spread (§3.5), so a clear offside pass is worth about losing the ball there; only a belief error or the softmax picks it. `possession_play`'s passer never picks an offside receiver. |
| 12, fouls | A keeper handles (saves) only inside his own penalty area (12.1); he never handles a team-mate's back pass or throw-in (12.2), and never holds the ball for eight seconds (12.3). A tackle counts only ball-first: the tackler is nearer the ball than the carrier, our stand-in for a careless challenge (12.1; `press_escape`, the only scenario with tackles). Players are points with no contact model, so the sim has no other fouls to call. |
| 13–17, restarts | The only simulated restart is `corner_blind_side`'s corner (`Scenario.restart`): the ball in the corner area, part of it over the arc being enough, and opponents 9.15 m from the arc until it is taken (17.1; `Referee.keep_back`). The receiver of a corner cannot be offside (11.3). Checks for the others (13.2–13.3, 15.1, 16.1) are in `laws.restart_breaches`; throw-ins, goal kicks and kick-offs between possessions are stitches (§3.1), and `eval/laws.py` lists what the Laws award and to which team; the next open-play passage starts with that team (§3.1). |

  `possession_play_away`, 2026-10-22). Every other hero
  gate is unchanged; `blind_side_run` flips 33/40 (was 39/40, offside) and its scan lens wins
  34/40.
- **Not yet.** Live match stats skip a pass that ends in an offside call (§4.6: their outcomes
  are complete, intercepted, out).

## 4. Analytics kernels (`analytics/`)
Every kernel: numpy reference + unit tests. GLSL versions in `web/` must match the numpy reference
within tolerance on fixed snapshots.

### 4.1 Time to intercept (TTI)
Shaw-style simplification of Spearman: the player keeps current velocity for reaction time τ_r,
then runs at v_max to the target. Defaults (*tunable*): τ_r = 0.7 s, v_max = 5 m/s (a deliberately
conservative model parameter, not real sprint speed). Targets: grid cells (pitch control) and
moving players/ball (pressing). The kernel counts arrivals after the reaction time only; the
decision model adds the run-in during it (§3.5) on top of the kernel, which stays as it is.

### 4.2 Pitch control
Closed-form approximation:
`P_home(x) = 1 / (1 + exp(−π / (√3 · σ) · (T_away(x) − T_home(x))))`,
where `T_team(x)` = min over that team's players of TTI and σ ≈ 0.45 s (*tunable*).
Backend grid 105 × 68 at 1 m; per-pixel in the shader. May refine toward Spearman's full
integration later if time allows.

### 4.3 Pressing intensity (after Bekkers)
Defender→attacker and defender→ball TTI, mapped to a probability with a logistic. Per-frame matrix;
aggregate pressure on the ball carrier.
- `p[d, a] = logistic(π / (√3 σ) · (T − TTI[d, a]))` with T = 1.5 s, σ = 0.45 s (*tunable*).
- The carrier's column is the max of pressure on his body and on the ball.
- Aggregate on a player: `1 − Π_d (1 − p[d, a])`. No pressure while the ball is free.
- Also from the paper: targets 1 s ahead (`r + v`), heading penalty `|v|·β/π`; active filter off.

### 4.4 Vision map and observed-space metrics
Per player: `V(x) = FOV(x; head_yaw, speed) × (1 − Occlusion(x))`. Metrics:
- `observed_defended_share` = Σ V·P_def / Σ P_def over opponent-controlled space — the share of
  the opponents' space he actually saw.
- `time_since_last_scan_at_reception`, `scans_in_window(t−10 s, t)`.
- `belief_error`: mean distance between believed and true positions of the relevant players.
- **Blind-side alert** (*M4*, live): perception made visible during play, not only in replays.
  Computed per frame from tracking only — observed `head_yaw` plus positions, the same inputs as
  the scan detector (§4.7), never the sim's internal beliefs — so it would run on real skeletal
  tracking. For an observer `o` and a target `t`, the alert is on when:
  - `t` has been outside `o`'s visible region (V < 0.5, §4.4) for ≥ 1.0 s;
  - `t` has moved ≥ 3 m since `o` last saw him, at ≥ 4 m/s;
  - `t` is within 35 m of `o` (25 m missed H9, who runs 22–30 m from H8 in `blind_side_run`).
  Observers are the ball carrier and the intended receiver (targets: teammates making runs) and
  the four defenders nearest the ball (targets: opponents). Hysteresis: on for at least 0.4 s; it
  clears when `o` sees `t` again. All thresholds are *tunable*; the values above are the starting
  point, and the tuned defaults and their tables are in `eval/reports/blind_side.md`
  (`analytics/blind_side.py`, M4). Deterministic, no LLM, within the
  live overlay budget (§2). An alert that ends in a pass to its target within 3 s is a moment
  trigger (§5.1).

### 4.5 Expected threat (xT)
16 × 12 grid fitted on synthetic event data with our own Markov implementation (value iteration,
about 100 lines). socceraction is not used: it is no longer maintained and would be a dependency
for a few lines of maths. Cache the grid per sim version.
*Implemented (2026-10-20):* `analytics/xt.py`, fitted by `eval/xt_fit.py`.
- Data: `open_play` tuning seeds 0–5999 (0–1999 until 2026-10-22: with keepers on their spot,
  §3.2, they claim most balls in the goal area, whose central cells reach the 20 actions a fitted
  cell needs only from 6,000 seeds on) — `possession_play`'s shape, pressing and marking, but
  every ball carrier decides with the decision model (§3.5), so play reaches the box and ends in
  shots. The scripted scenarios cannot carry the fit: `possession_play` and `blind_side_run` have
  no shots at all. A shot scores its expected goals under the sim's shot model (the decision
  model's xG; the engine has no shot outcome model); a lost ball is worth 0. Open play is
  symmetric across the long axis, so each action also counts mirrored.
- Fallback: a cell with fewer than 20 actions keeps the smooth prior, moved down to the lowest
  fitted value (the prior's floor is a guess, the fit measures it); with fewer than 30 shots in
  all, the whole grid is the prior.
- Cache: `analytics/xt_grid.json` records the sim version, a hash of what open play does on
  probe seeds; `tests/test_xt.py` fails when the sim no longer matches. `xt_for` reads the grid,
  bilinear between cell centres, so a millimetre never jumps a cell boundary.
- Decisions keep the prior (§3.5); possession value, the lenses, moments and the Analyst's
  `get_xt` read the grid.

### 4.6 Pass metrics and speeds (live match stats)
The challenge's real-time match intelligence list: players named on screen with speed and
distance indicators at thresholds; pass distance, accuracy and difficulty; ball and shot speed.
Kernels in [analytics/stats.py](../analytics/stats.py) (numpy reference), the timeline in
[analytics/live_stats.py](../analytics/live_stats.py).

**Inputs: only what the live stream carries** (§2): positions and the ball in mm, the owner, and
the event feed (`pass`, `reception`, `interception`, `out`, `shot`). No sim velocities, beliefs
or pass outcomes from the sim's own attributes. A stitched match's possessions are tracking
segments: positions jump at a possession's first frame, so no speed, distance or pass spans one.
**Tackles are not modelled yet** outside the pressure hero (`press_escape`): in a stitched match
the ball changes hands only by interceptions and balls out of play, and the panel says so.

**Causal.** Every value at frame f uses frames ≤ f only (tested by truncating the input), so it
could run frame by frame on a live feed. Each cue carries the frame at which its value is final:
a pass at its reception, interception or out; a sprint at its first frame back below the threshold.

- **Speed.** Smoothed over the last 0.2 s: v(f) = |p(f) − p(f − k)| / (k·dt), k = min(5, frames
  since the segment began); 0 on a segment's first frame. The same for the ball. *Tunable.*
- **Thresholds.** High-speed running > 5.5 m/s (19.8 km/h), sprint > 7.0 m/s (25.2 km/h): the most
  common entry speeds for male professionals in the 30 studies of Gualtieri et al. (2023). A sprint
  is a run above 7.0 m/s lasting at least 0.4 s (10 frames), as the sprint moment (§5.1); that
  duration is ours, not a published value.
- **Distance covered.** The sum of frame-to-frame steps within a segment (the sim's tracking has
  no position noise; a real feed would be smoothed first). High-speed and sprint distance count
  the steps that end above each threshold. Milestones every 250 m (*tunable*).
- **Passes.** A pass event starts one; the first `reception`, `interception` or `out` after it
  resolves it. Attempted = resolved; completed = resolved by a teammate's reception (accuracy =
  completed / attempted). A pass still in flight when its segment ends, or when another kick
  follows, is not counted. Distance: from the ball at the kick to the ball at the resolution.
- **Ball and shot speed.** Release speed: the mean ball speed over the first 0.2 s after the kick
  (|b(k + m) − b(k)| / (m·dt), m = min(5, frames to the resolution)); shots too, once shot events
  exist (§3.3). The live ball speed is the smoothed speed above.
- **Pass difficulty** ∈ [0, 1], a fixed formula from our kernels, set before any check of it and
  not fitted to our outcomes:
  `difficulty = 0.4·lane + 0.2·passer + 0.2·receiver + 0.2·min(d / 50 m, 1)`.
  - *lane*: interception risk along the ball's path, after the physics-based pass model of
    Spearman et al. (2017), as the decision model's P(win ball) (§3.5) on the tracked state. Per
    opponent, the larger of two chances: he reacts and reaches a point of the path before the ball,
    `logistic(π/(√3·σ)·(τ − TTI))` maximised over the path (τ the ball's time to the point, TTI
    from his position and smoothed velocity at the kick, §4.1, σ = 0.45 s); or, before he reacts,
    the ball comes within the control radius (1 m) of his run, with 0.3 m of position spread (the
    run-in). Then `1 − Π (1 − p)` over opponents. The path is the one the ball travelled, so the
    rating describes the pass as played.
  - *passer*: pressing intensity on the passer at the kick (§4.3, the carrier's value).
  - *receiver*: pressing intensity on the receiver's body when the ball arrives (§4.3, without
    the ball term): the reception's player, or for a failed pass the pass event's intended
    receiver (0 when the feed names none).
  - *distance*: a 50 m pass scores 1 (*tunable*). The weights are a design choice: the lane, the
    direct way a pass fails, counts twice each other factor. Progression and line-breaking (ΔxT)
    are a pass's value, not its difficulty, and stay out.
  - Bands for viewers: < 0.25 routine, < 0.5 moderate, < 0.75 hard, else very hard.
- **Names.** Fictional names by player id ([sim/roster.py](../sim/roster.py)), never real
  players'; the export is unchanged.
  - The same names appear everywhere a player is named: the stats, the narration and its voice,
    and the app's own chips and labels (the lineup, the eyes picker, the HUD, moments, Your call,
    the director).
  - Narration renders his short name ("J. Ferriday") as a slot value (agents/context.py
    `names`). The Verifier keeps names, surnames and the raw ids out of free text, with ASCII-only
    word boundaries so a name next to a Chinese character is caught too.
  - The two clubs are named too: Kestrel Bay (home) and Corvane Vale (away), both invented. The
    recap's claims still say Home and Away (its cache key holds their templates); the API names
    the clubs when it serves a recap.
  - Ids stay the data: claims, evidence, events and URLs.
  - A breakdown, recap or what-if answer cached before names were rendered is renamed when it is
    read ([api/names.py](../api/names.py)). The Verifier allowed an id into a line only as a slot
    value, so this is the same text rendered with names, and nothing is generated again.
  - The web app mirrors the roster in [web/src/data/roster.ts](../web/src/data/roster.ts), and
    `tests/test_roster_web.py` keeps the two equal.
  - The what-if parser understands the names too ("What if he passed to Annersley?"), and the
    export's suggested questions use them ([sim/export.py](../sim/export.py)).
- **Serving.** `GET /api/matches/<id>/stats` (pydantic `MatchStats`: player and team totals, cues,
  definitions); `?frame=F` is the snapshot at frame F, `?tracks=true` adds per-frame ball speed,
  speeds above 19.8 km/h and per-second distance checkpoints for viewers. The live stream sends each
  cue as a `stat` message on its frame (§2), timecoded for a graphics engine. The Analyst's MCP
  tool `get_live_stats` serves the same numbers (§6).

### 4.7 Scan detector
Input: the `head_yaw` time series plus the player's and the ball's positions (needed for the ball
direction); no sim-internal state. Detect scans as excursions away from the ball direction
whose head angular velocity exceeds a threshold. The traditional 125°/s threshold is known to
over-count versus video annotation, so sweep 125–300°/s and choose by F1 against sim ground truth.
Report a precision / recall / F1 table in `eval/`, with and without the nuisance head motion
of §3.4.

*Ingest from limb tracking (optional; it goes with the skeleton view, item 0 of the §9 cut
order).* An offline adapter shows that the Ingest stage could accept body keypoints, the kind of
data limb tracking reports; nothing in the API or the app calls it yet.
- **Schema:** a generic open one, a named subset of the COCO keypoints (nose, ears, shoulders,
  hips), in metres in pitch coordinates. It travels one frame per message (pydantic
  `KeypointFrame`).
- **Adapter** ([analytics/keypoints.py](../analytics/keypoints.py), no sim state): recovers each
  player's position (hip midpoint), head yaw and torso yaw.
  - Head yaw: the ear axis and the nose's offset from the ear midpoint, combined with
    inverse-variance weights.
  - Torso yaw: the shoulder and hip axes, combined the same way.
  - An untracked keypoint holds the last estimate. (Collapsed or infinite points count as
    untracked.)
  - The scan detector then runs on that head yaw unchanged.
- **Synthetic stream** ([sim/keypoints.py](../sim/keypoints.py)): keypoints placed from a run's
  truth.
  - Noise is independent and Gaussian, keyed by (match seed, `keypoint_noise`, frame).
  - Its size is the §3.4 sensor noise carried into keypoint space: 4.7 mm per axis makes the
    recovered head yaw as noisy as the sim's tracked yaw (2°).
  - Generating a stream changes no run and no export.
  - Limbs beyond shoulders and hips are not generated: the sim has none.

### 4.8 Tactics cards (2026-10-21)
A teammate's tactics knowledge base (tactic → mechanism → data evidence → fan explanation) needs
events the sim does not have for most rows (tackles, crosses, open play that shoots). Two cards
are built from what it records; the rest of the table and what each row needs are in
[docs/TACTICS.md](TACTICS.md). Kernels in [analytics/tactics.py](../analytics/tactics.py), the
sentences in [explain/tactics.py](../explain/tactics.py), `GET /api/matches/<id>/tactics`.

**Inputs: the live stream, as §4.6.** Pass cues from the stats timeline give the outcome from the
feed and the pressing intensity on the passer at the kick. Tracking gives the ball and the
players. The feed gives the intended receiver (`to`) and the shots. Each possession's restart
comes from the export. The one exception is the decision model's recorded options (§3.5), which
are sim records, labelled `sim` on screen.

- **Why build from the back?** A build-up pass is a resolved pass kicked in the passer's own half.
  - *Under pressure*: pressing intensity on the passer ≥ 0.7 at the kick, the lexicon's heavy
    pressure.
  - *Long*: to a teammate 32 m or more away at the kick (35 yards; the length to the intended
    receiver, so a long ball cut out early is still long).
  - *Line-breaking*: completed past ≥ 2 outfield opponents, as §5.1 counts them, on tracking.
  - *xT added*: completed, xT where received minus where played; lost, minus the opponent's xT
    where they won it, minus where played (§4.5's grid). *Handed over*: the opponent's xT
    there.
  - Per team: all, under pressure and not, short and long, short under pressure, line-breaking,
    lost. The card's team is the one with more own-half passes (ties: home).
  - Where the sim recorded a decision in the carrier's own half: his choice, his best short and
    best long pass by the decision model's value, and his safest pass.
- **Why focus on set pieces?** A corner is a possession whose restart is a corner. It is
  *played* when its scenario begins with the corner (`sim.match.SET_PIECES`); otherwise the stitch
  restarts it as open play.
  - Shots after it: the corner team's shots in that possession. Their xG is the sim's distance
    model (`sim.decision.distance_xg`).
  - The match's shots and xG, and the share from set pieces.
  - A passage's *peak*: the completed pass received where it was worth the most xT to the
    receiving team. The card compares the best corner's peak with the open-play passages'.
- **Sentences.** One per card, a claim (§5.3) with `en` and `zh` templates. Its slots are filled
  from the card a viewer sees. `tactics_stat` measures the card again from the run's tracking and
  events, with the stitched run's possessions, and the Verifier checks every slot before the line
  renders. Percentages and decimals are checked to half a unit of the last digit shown.
- **Honest limits, on the card.**
  - Open-play passing is scripted (§3.6): the rates describe the sim's script under its press.
  - A corner's possession ends after its cutback, before any shot (§3.1): set-piece xG is zero
    by construction.
  - Open play rarely reaches the final third, so the peak comparison favours the corner.
- **Where.** The analyst's stats panel and the end-of-match recap (§7).

## 5. Explain layer (`explain/`)

### 5.1 Moment detection
Rule-based detectors over events + analytics: goal, big chance, line-breaking pass, successful
counter-press, sprint tag (live overlay only), and *M4* a pass to the target of a blind-side alert
(§4.4). Each moment yields a `MomentCard` — the shared state for the agents: ids, time window,
involved players, key metrics, candidate lenses.
- *Implemented (M2):*
  - line-breaking pass: completed, past ≥ 2 outfield opponents;
  - decision after a scan: a reception, then the receiver's decision, with a scan ending ≤ 2 s
    before the reception;
  - sprint: > 7.0 m/s for ≥ 0.4 s, live overlay only.
  - decision under pressure (`pressed_decision`, 2026-10-20): a decision on the ball with
    pressing intensity on the carrier ≥ 0.7, the lexicon's own threshold for "under heavy
    pressure"; first in merge priority, so a pressed decision after a scan is one card with both
    tags.
  Goal, big chance and counter-press need shot outcomes and turnovers (M4).
  - a marker's look (`marker_scan`, 2026-10-21), for a hero with no decision on the ball
    (`corner_blind_side`): a defender's scan at the man he marks, before a pass to that man. All
    of these hold: the pass's intended receiver is in the defender's own third when it is played;
    the defender is his nearest outfield opponent then, goal-side of him; the scan starts at most
    2 s before the pass with the receiver within 5 m (the lexicon's `unmarked` distance: he is
    marking him); the receiver has been out of the defender's sight for the 1.2 s before it (the
    blind-side alert's unseen time, §4.4) and is seen during it. The anchor is the pass's
    resolution, where the look pays off or not. It fires once on every `corner_blind_side` design
    seed, on the key scan, and never on 60 seeds of `blind_side_run`, `press_escape` and
    `possession_play` (`tests/test_corner_breakdown.py`; 300 were checked while it was built).
    Open play can have one, as it should: a defender checking the man he marks before a pass to
    him. It is then an ordinary moment, never a hero card.
    `explain.moments.hero_card` returns a scenario hero's moment: the card on his decision, or
    his `marker_scan`.
- Detections about the same action merge into one card with several tags. Card ids look like
  `m:decision_after_scan:H8:0075`.
- **He thought vs it was.** For a decision moment, the card records P(success) of the chosen
  option on the player's belief and on the true state, at the action frame — also for the
  counterfactual run's choice. The freeze at the pass shows both ("he thought 0.05, it was 0.80").
  "It was" is the same option (same receiver) re-scored by the decision model on the true state
  (true positions and velocities, belief age 0), so its target follows the receiver's true run.
  Holding his believed target fixed would score 0 whenever he misplaced the receiver, however open
  the receiver was; the export keeps that value as `p_success_true_same_target`.
  Belief-side values are `computed` claims, counterfactual-run values are `counterfactual` claims.
- A card names its anchor events (`reception`, `last_scan`, `action`, `completion`), so claims
  anchor on `<card>.<anchor>` (§5.3).
- `content_hash()` keys the breakdown cache (§2).

### 5.2 Lens selection by counterfactual impact
For each moment, compute candidate explanations. All three deltas are in xT of the acting team.
`scan` and `space` share one value function, evaluated on the true state at the decision:
`V(option) = PC·xT(target) − (1 − PC)·xT_opp(target)` (shots keep xG).
- `scan`: drop the most informative scan — the one whose sightings move the decision-time belief
  about the eventual target most — and re-simulate (§3.7).
  Δ = V(factual choice) − V(counterfactual choice). The realised outcome difference is recorded as
  `realized_delta_xt`, but not used for selection: it is an outcome, not an expectation.
- `space`: Δ = V(chosen) − V(best alternative).
- `pressure`: expected turnover cost = pressing intensity × (xT_own + xT_opp at the ball).
  Δ = cost on the actor at the decision − cost after the action.

Pick the lens with the largest absolute delta; exact ties go `scan > space > pressure`. Record all
three in the MomentCard.

A placed shot (`shot:near` / `shot:far`, Module A) is worth its chance of a goal on the truth:
P(on target) × (1 − E[P(save)]) with the keeper where he really is, the decision model's own value
re-scored on the true state. Its xG-like value on his belief would make the look worth less than
nothing whenever his stale picture flattered the near post. Without the look the striker picks
the other placement, which is also the space lens's best alternative, so on `far_post_finish`
Δscan equals Δspace and the tie goes to `scan`.

A marker's look (`marker_scan`) has no decision on the ball, so only `scan` applies; `space` and
`pressure` are recorded as unavailable. Its value is the pressure lens's currency from the
defending side: the expected turnover where the pass to his man resolves, pressing intensity on
the receiver × (xT_own + xT_opp at the ball), 1 × stake if the defence won the ball.
Δ = that with the marker's most informative scan − that with the scan dropped and re-simulated.
Pitch control at the ball says little here: the receiver is on the ball either way.

Without the scan, the player chooses one of the alternatives, so Δscan ≥ Δspace whenever the scan
changed the decision. The scan lens therefore wins exactly when the scan was decisive. An
uninformative scan scores 0, and then `space` wins (tested). On `blind_side_run`, 80 seeds
(0–39 and 100–139): `scan` is selected in 69/80 with the fitted grid and with the smooth prior
(80/80 before the run was made onside, §3.9), Δscan ≥ Δspace in 80/80, and the median margin over
`space` is about 0.014 xT with the fitted grid (0.003 with the smooth prior;
[eval/reports/xt_fit.md](../eval/reports/xt_fit.md)).
On `press_escape`, fresh held-out seeds 200–239: `pressure` is selected in 40/40 with the fitted
grid. With the smooth prior it was 38/40 and `space` in 2/40 — the seeds where the softmax drew
a poor long ball (probability 0.02) and the space lens measured what the best pass was worth
instead; `scan` never (H4's own scans change nothing,
Δscan ≈ 0). Before the decision model saw the presser's run-in (§3.5), H4 carried into the press
in 7 of seeds 100–139 and `space` won 9/40 there (now 1/40). Report:
`eval/reports/hero_lens_press_escape.md`.

### 5.3 Claims and verifier
Every factual sentence on screen comes from a `Claim`:

```json
{
  "id": "c17",
  "kind": "observed",
  "template": "{player} scanned {n_scans} times in the {window_s} seconds before receiving.",
  "slots": {"player": {"ref": "H8"}, "n_scans": {"value": 3}, "window_s": {"value": 6}},
  "predicate": {
    "op": "eq",
    "lhs": {"fn": "count_events",
            "args": {"type": "scan", "player": "H8", "from": -6.0, "to": 0.0, "anchor": "m12.reception"}},
    "rhs": 3
  },
  "evidence": ["e4512", "e4519", "e4533"]
}
```

- The verifier evaluates predicates against the event store → `supported | refuted | inconclusive`.
  Only `supported` claims render.
- Narration text uses slot placeholders; the renderer fills numbers from verified values. LLMs
  never emit digits; with a model, they do not write narration text at all (§6, anchored
  narration).
- **No placeholder survives.** A `{` or `}` left in a rendered line (a malformed or unknown
  placeholder) is a `placeholder` violation, so it never airs as literal braces.
- **Number words count as digits.** The render check rejects any number that did not come from a
  slot: ASCII digits; English number words (`one`…`twenty`, tens, `hundred`, `twice`, `thrice`,
  `dozen`, `half`, …); in `zh`, a Chinese numeral followed by a measure word (三次, 两名, 五米, …).
  Idiomatic 一 (一个, 一脚, 一眼, 一下) is allowlisted — banning 一 outright is impossible in Chinese.
- **Qualitative claims are claims too.** "Unmarked" or "under heavy pressure" carry no digit but
  are still factual. A small lexicon maps such terms to predicates, e.g. `unmarked` ⇒ nearest
  opponent > 5 m; `under heavy pressure` ⇒ pressing intensity on the carrier ≥ 0.7; `blind side`
  ⇒ outside the vision cone at the last scan; `lost his man` ⇒ a marker more than 5 m from the
  man he marks (zh 跟丢) (all *tunable*). A lexicon term may appear only in a
  line that references a claim verifying it. Lines without a claim reference are limited to
  connective phrases.
- Predicate ops: `eq, ne, lt, le, gt, ge, approx(tol), exists, before`.
- Functions: `count_events, metric_at, metric_delta, event_attr, distance, scans_in_window,
  observed_share, cf_delta`, and for a marker's look `seconds_between` and
  `turnover_at_resolution`, with the metrics `time_unseen` (since he last saw his man) and
  `sighting_error` (his belief rebuilt from sightings, for a player with no recorded decision);
  for a placed shot the metrics `shot_success` (his chance of a goal, on his belief or the truth)
  and `near_side_offset` (the keeper's offset toward the shooter's near post).
- Every verdict is written to the claims audit log (Cosmos DB) with inputs and timestamps.
- **Claim kinds, shown on screen.** Every claim is one of:
  - `observed`: an event or tracking fact (a scan count, a pass outcome);
  - `computed`: an analytics metric (pitch control, xT, belief error, pressing);
  - `counterfactual`: from re-simulating the moment with an intervention (dropping a scan). It is
    causal inside the sim only (§3.5), and the label makes that visible.
  The viewer labels each line's evidence with its kind, so facts, metrics and what-ifs never blur.
- **Banned evaluative terms.** Judgements that cannot be written as a predicate never appear in
  narration, in any persona or language — e.g. `mistake`, `error`, `dominated`, `controlled the
  game`, `certain goal`, `should have`, `lazy`, `world-class`, `ball-watching` (pundits' blame
  for a marker who lost his man); in `zh` 失误, 统治比赛, 必进, 本该, 偷懒, 盯球不盯人. A goal on the counter is not by itself a defensive mistake, possession is not control, and
  xG is not a certain goal. The render check rejects these like an unslotted number.
- **Render payload.** What a viewer receives for one breakdown. Each line lists its claim ids;
  each claim carries its kind, verdict, filled values, evidence event ids and a seek frame, so any
  line's evidence can be opened and the replay jumped there (§7):

```json
{"moment": "m12", "persona": "analyst", "language": "en",
 "lines": [{"id": "L2", "text": "H8 checked his shoulder once before the ball arrived.", "claims": ["c17"]}],
 "claims": [{"id": "c17", "kind": "observed", "verdict": "supported", "values": {"n_scans": 1},
             "evidence": ["scan:H8:0050"], "seek_frame": 50}]}
```

## 6. Agents (`agents/`)
Microsoft Agent Framework (Python) workflow; models via Microsoft Foundry with structured outputs;
tools exposed by our own MCP server.

| Agent | Input | Output (JSON schema) | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Director | MomentCard stream, budget | `BreakdownRequest` (moment, personas, languages, priority) | rate-limits breakdowns; picks replay slots; the audiences come from the request, the model sets only priority, which is the breakdown's budget (below) |
| Analyst | BreakdownRequest + MCP tools | `Findings` (lens, ranked facts as Claims) | tools: `get_moment`, `get_vision`, `get_pitch_control`, `run_counterfactual`, `get_xt`, `get_pressing` |
| Telestrator | Findings | `TelestrationPlan` | camera moves, freeze/rewind, arrows, zones, ghosts, POV segments, timecodes |
| Commentator | Findings + persona + language | anchored lines (claim id and/or connective), become `Script` lines with `speaker: "pbp"` | play-by-play voice: what happened, short and paced |
| Co-commentator | Findings + Commentator's lines + persona + language | anchored lines, become `Script` lines with `speaker: "color"` | analysis voice: why it happened, the perception story; replies to the play-by-play |
| Verifier | Script + Claims | verdicts; approve or request a rewrite | deterministic code wrapped as a workflow step |
| Voice | approved Script + TelestrationPlan | audio (Azure Speech TTS, SSML) | one voice per speaker and language; cached by content hash |

**Two voices.** The two narration agents write one interleaved `Script`, each line tagged with its
`speaker`. Both voices in a language are male: play-by-play `en-US-GuyNeural` and
`zh-CN-YunjianNeural`, colour `en-US-DavisNeural` and `zh-CN-YunyeNeural`. If either fails, a
single-narrator template takes over (failure policy below).

**Persona rules** (`agents/persona_rules.py`). The red-team review found the cached model scripts
of different personas were the same claims reordered (casual heard expected threat and no
counterfactual; coach no counterfactual). Each persona now has claims it may not cite, groups of
claims its script (both voices together) must cite one of, and a number budget:

| Persona | Must cite (one of each group) | May not cite | Numbers |
|---|---|---|---|
| `casual` | the counterfactual (`cf_pass`, `cf_carry_lost`, `cf_lost_runner`, `cf_unmarked`, `cf_shot`) | xT and probabilities (`cf_value`, `cf_shot_value`, `pass_success`, `cf_pass_success`, `shot_thought_vs_was`) | ≤ 2 |
| `analyst` | the look's value (`cf_value`, `cf_shot_value`); belief error; scan timing (scan lens) | – | – |
| `player_fan` | his own look (scan lens); his decision (`pass_to_runner`, `runner_received`, `shot_placement`, `stayed_with_runner`) | – | – |
| `coach` | the counterfactual; a coaching point (scan timing, `presser_distance`, `pressure_at_pass`, `keeper_near_side`) | – | – |
| `audio_description` | a spatial claim first (`reception_zone`, `scan_side`, `blind_side`) | xT, probabilities, shares and pressing percentages | ≤ 2 |

A group is required only when the moment has a supported claim in it, and a scan group only on
the scan lens. Numbers are counted as the numeric slots of the cited claims (`{n:times}` counts;
vocabulary slots do not). Enforcement, in order: the forbidden ids are not in that persona's
schema enum, so the model cannot pick them; the prompt carries the rules (`must_include`,
`max_numbers`, `start_with`, and for the co-commentator `still_missing`); tidy drops a forbidden
line; the Verifier checks every script and sends one that breaks a rule back to both voices,
whole, with the problems (a rewrite round, at most two); after the last round `repair` makes it
comply with verified template lines (drops forbidden lines, adds the first supported claim of a
missing group in story order, brings a spatial line first, trims numeric lines that cover no
group). The templates go through the same `repair`. A recap tells several moments and keeps only
the forbidden ids.
The narration ablation (`eval/narration_ablation.py`) and the crew-vs-single eval
(`eval/crew_vs_single.py`) run the Verifier with these script checks off (`LineVerifier`, line
checks only), as when their reports were measured: with them on, the single agent's one call is
redrafted whole and its calls and rewrites are no longer the ones the report counts. Arm D of
crew-vs-single is therefore the crew with the line-check Verifier, not the deployed one.

**Delivery tiers.** Each moment gets `calm | excited | clutch` from deterministic rules on the
MomentCard (lens |Δ|, outcome, phase), never from an LLM. Each line also has a role. `open` is a
play-by-play connective (no claim). `build` is any other play-by-play line. `peak` is play-by-play
that cites the moment's decision event — an evidence id whose player and frame are the last two
`:` fields of the moment id (`m:<kind>:<actor>:<anchor frame>`, frames as ints). `why` is every
co-commentator line. Role and tier pick a speaking style, best first, the first the voice has;
otherwise prosody only. `styledegree` is set only when a style is used. Prosody (rate, pitch)
always wraps the words.

| role | tier | styles, best first | styledegree | rate | pitch |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| open | any | sports-commentary, cheerful | – | +6% | +3% |
| build | any | sports-commentary-excited, excited, cheerful | – | +8% | +3% |
| peak | calm | as build | – | +8% | +3% |
| peak | excited | sports-commentary-excited, excited, cheerful | 1.5 | +10% | +4% |
| peak | clutch | sports-commentary-excited, excited, cheerful | 2 | +14% | +6% |
| why | any | cheerful, excited | – | +2% | +2% |

Decisive words (the lexicon terms, "that look", the shoulder checks, 那一眼, 回头看了一眼) are
their own louder prosody span, and a counterfactual line takes a 250 ms break after the first
comma. Audio description ignores the role: neutral styles only (`narration-relaxed`,
`narration-professional`, `calm`, `newscast`), rate −10%, no emphasis and no break, never
`excited` or `cheerful`. Styles are the static table `VOICE_STYLES`;
`python -m infra.voices` checks it against Azure Speech. A cached breakdown is re-voiced on read:
cue keys change, the breakdown key does not.

**Failure policy.** Per-step timeout (≈ 8 s, *tunable*), one retry, then a template fallback for
that step. Refuted claim → rewrite by the agent that wrote the line (max 2) → drop the line. Every handoff is traced to
Application Insights.

*Implemented (M3, `agents/`):*
- One Agent Framework workflow; each executor handles one typed message, and the Verifier has two
  outgoing edges: back to Narration (rewrite request) or on to Voice (approved scripts).

  concurrently. Voice places lines on the plan's clock, so it waits for both the validated plan
  and the approved scripts. That was a fan-in edge until the failure drill; it is now a join in
  Voice, so a re-run step's result is voiced again. The framework runs in supersteps, so a
  rewrite round starts after the Telestrator has finished. `trace` keeps the order Director,
  Analyst, Telestrator, narration, Verifier, Voice whichever lane finishes first.
- Models get claims as templates with slot names, never raw text to copy numbers from. The Analyst
  returns claim ids only, constrained to the supported ids by an enum in its JSON schema; a reply
  outside the enum fails validation and the Analyst's template takes over.
- **Anchored narration: the model directs, the verified templates speak.** Each narration line the
  model returns is a supported claim id, an allowed connective ("Watch this.", "注意看。"), or both;
  both are enums in the schema, so structured outputs cannot carry free text. A claim speaks its
  verified template, a connective itself (at most two per voice), and the Verifier still checks
  every line. *Why:* the first run against `gpt-4.1-mini` (M0, seed 3) produced free-text lines
  with broken grammar ("1 times"), repeated claims, invented player names, and a malformed
  placeholder (`{window_s|second}`) that rendered as literal braces. With anchored lines and the
  enum Analyst, two reruns aired 22 and 24 lines in four scripts (casual and analyst × en and zh)
  with no rewrites, no drops, no invented names, and every headline claim spoken.
- **The Director's priority is the breakdown's budget** (`agents/budget.py`, 2026-10-21). The
  agents after it read it: at high priority the Analyst inspects the moment with our MCP tools;
  at normal and low it answers from the claims in its prompt, one request instead of a tool loop.
  The priority also buys the replay a slot, 20, 16 or 12 s, never shorter than the moment's
  template plan: the Telestrator is told it, and a plan that runs longer airs as the template
  (`duration_s: over_slot`). With the Director on its template, the rule on the lens delta sets
  the priority and the budget follows. *Why:* a red-team read found the priority was written and
  never read. `tests/test_director_budget.py`.
- Telestrator: Azure structured outputs reject the `oneOf` a discriminated union produces, so the
  model fills `PlanDraft` (a plain `anyOf` union) and `to_plan()` applies the full checks.

  right after two others hit the deployment's rate limit (capacity 20: 20 requests and 20K tokens

- *Any lens (2026-10-20).* The counterfactual (`cf1`) is the scenario's own intervention: the run
  without the key scan, or for a pressure moment the hero forced to keep the ball. Pressure

- *A marker's look (2026-10-21, `corner_blind_side`).* The hero never has the ball. His claims

- *A placed shot (2026-10-21, `far_post_finish`).* Claims: the look before the ball arrived

  truth. A what-if pass from a striker who shot cites the decision as its evidence, since the
  re-simulated pass is not an event of the real run.
- Every model step has a deterministic template (`agents/fallbacks.py`), so the crew runs end to end
  offline. A Telestrator plan is validated with the same rules as `web/` and replaced by the
  template plan when invalid. The reasons are public (`trace[].notes`, `plan_errors`), so each is a
  location and a fixed code (`steps.3 (scan_marker): unknown_scan`, `duration_s:
  less_than_equal`), never a value or time the model wrote (§5.3).
- **Failure drill** (2026-10-20, for the "recover from failures" point of Best Multi-Agent
  System). It makes the failure policy visible and adds recovery from Agent Framework workflow
  checkpoints.
  - *Take down.* `POST /api/breakdowns` with `fail: ["telestrator" | "analyst" | "narration" |
    "director"]` runs the crew once with those agents down. A downed agent's chat client
    (`agents/llm.py` `Outage`) raises `AgentDown` on every call, so no model is called for that
    step. The step then takes the normal path above: the call, one retry, its template.
    - The run has its own key, never a normal cache key, and every drill runs the crew afresh.
    - A drill counts against the per-client rate limit and a daily cap: with a model, the daily
      model cap; offline, its own cap of 200 drills and bring-backs per UTC day. Past any of
      them: 429.
  - *Waiting.* A step that fell back after an error (a drill or a real outage) sends a workflow
    request (`AgentDown`, `request_info`): the run ends with its output aired and the request
    pending.
  - *Bring back.* `POST /api/breakdowns/<key>/bring-back` with `{"step": ...}` resumes the
    workflow from its latest checkpoint with the answer (`AgentBack`). That step runs again on
    its checkpointed input, and only the steps that read its output follow:
    - the Telestrator: Telestrator → Voice;
    - narration: narration → Verifier → Voice;
    - the Analyst or the Director: everything downstream.
    Voice joins the two lanes in code (by Briefing revision) instead of a fan-in edge, so a
    revised plan or script is voiced again. The new breakdown replaces the drill's under the same
    key. A drill can be brought back at most 5 times, so a step that keeps failing for real
    cannot be retried forever. One crew run per key at a time: a second bring-back of the same
    step waits, then answers 409 because the step no longer waits.
  - *Interrupted runs.* Every crew run in the API checkpoints at each superstep
    (`FileCheckpointStorage` under `TTE_CACHE_DIR/checkpoints/`). Each executor checkpoints its last
    input and its trace entry. A breakdown interrupted mid-workflow, by a restart too, resumes
    from its last completed superstep on the next identical request. Its checkpoints are deleted
    once the result is cached. A checkpoint that no longer resumes, for example one left by older
    code, is deleted and the run starts over, so it cannot fail its key on every request.
  - *Where drills live.* A drill is one directory on the replica's disk: its record (the latest
    result, the agents still down, the bring-backs so far) and its checkpoints. Nothing is kept
    in memory or written to Cosmos DB, and only the 50 most recently used drills are kept.
  - *Limits.* Every restart drops the drills, and so does every redeploy: each green run of CI
    on `main` rolls a new revision onto a fresh disk. Bring-back then answers 404, and the drill
    has to be run again. A drill whose checkpoints are missing answers 410.
  - *Web.* The breakdown panel's Producer desk lists the six steps with their source (model,
    template or code, from `StepTrace.source`), the error class and the attempts. It has "Take
    down" toggles per agent, "Bring back" on each waiting step, and the breakdown's trace id
    (`BreakdownResult.trace_id`, operation_Id in Application Insights).
  - *Tracing.* A bring-back is its own trace (`tte.bring_back`, `tte.resumed`); its step spans
    are only the steps that ran again.
  - *Evidence:* `tests/test_crew_recovery.py`, `tests/test_api_drill.py`,

    - After its bring-back the fake gets exactly one call: the Telestrator's. The other steps'
      trace entries and the narration payload are unchanged.
    - An API process killed during narration resumes after a restart without calling the
      Director or the Analyst again.
- **Crew trace** (2026-10-21, for the Best Multi-Agent System criteria: distinct roles, shared
  state and handoffs, recovery, an outcome no single agent makes). Every crew run records its
  own timeline as the workflow runs (`agents/crewtrace.py`), with no model call: per step its
  start and end (ms since the run started), the typed message it handled and the ones it sent
  (a summary and the JSON), the shared state it read and wrote, its model calls (or its
  template, or code), the Analyst's MCP tool calls with the model's arguments and our server's
  results, and the Verifier's rejections with the rewrites they asked for. Run events show the
  drill: an agent taken down, its fallback, its request to be brought back, every checkpoint
  with the state it saved, and the bring-back resuming from one.
  - *Shared state, as built.* Reads are the evidence every agent works from (`MomentContext`,
    verified before any model runs), noted as each step reads it. Writes are each step's output
    on the workflow's state (Agent Framework `State`: `request`, `findings`, `plan`, `drafts`,
    `verdicts`, `scripts`, `voice`; the recap's Director also writes `evidence`, its focus),
    which every checkpoint saves. The handoffs themselves stay typed messages on the edges.
  - *Stored and served.* The API stores the trace with the cached breakdown, recap or drill and
    serves it by key (`GET /api/breakdowns/<key>/trace`, `GET /api/recaps/<key>/trace`); a POST
    answer says whether it has one (`traced`). A bring-back continues the drill's trace with a
    new run; a run resumed after an interruption continues the trace saved on its checkpoint.
    Breakdowns cached before traces were recorded answer `recorded: false` and the web says
    "trace not recorded for this cached run". The cache keys did not move (`PIPELINE_VERSION`
    unchanged); `infra.prewarm` reports such views as "no crew trace", and
    `--regenerate-degraded` runs them again.
  - *Public, like the breakdown.* Ids, counts, verified templates with their placeholders,
    reason codes and error classes; model text only where the breakdown already shows it (the
    Director's reason, the Analyst's angle) and in tool-call arguments, labelled as the model's.
    Every time and duration is telemetry and the web labels it so; each step has its span id,
    and each run its trace id (operation_Id in Application Insights).
  - *Web.* "Crew trace" on the Producer desk, in the breakdown's head and under the recap opens
    the timeline (`web/src/crewtrace/`): a lane per step in the graph's order for each run, bars
    filled by who answered (model, template, code), the drill's events, and the picked step's
    details.
  - *Evidence:* `tests/test_crew_trace.py`, `tests/test_api_trace.py`,
    `web/src/crewtrace/crewtrace.test.tsx` (on a fixture the backend recorded:
    `uv run python -m agents.crewtrace`).

  answered with a fixed "not something he could do here" line. Latency as a breakdown (§2).
- Voice speaks four roles (`open`, `build`, `peak`, `why`) on two male voices per language
  (play-by-play `en-US-GuyNeural` / `zh-CN-YunjianNeural`, colour `en-US-DavisNeural` /

- **Key-moment beat** ([web/src/beat/](../web/src/beat/)): each key scan plays like a broadcast
  highlight. Free play and the live stream ease into slow motion (about 0.4×) as the scan begins,
  hold for a beat (≈ 0.35 s) when the player the look is for first comes into the hero's sight,

  side (the sim's field of view is wider than 95°). The live playhead may fall behind the stream

  the frame only: no position, event or number changes.

## 8. Platform and deploy
- **Budget.** The organisers provide no Azure credit (rules checked 2026-10-19), so everything runs
  on the Azure for Students credit until 2026-11-10. Free tiers where they exist: Speech F0,
  the Application Insights free allowance, the Container Apps consumption
  grant. Images go to Azure Container Registry Basic (about $0.17/day): while the repo is private,
  a GHCR image would need a personal access token, but ACR is pulled with the app's managed
  identity, so no secret is involved.
  - LLM spend is bounded by the breakdown cache (§2), the per-client rate limit, and a global daily
    cap on model-backed regenerations (`TTE_DAILY_MODEL_CAP`). Past the cap, the service serves the
    cache or the template.
  - Speech spend: the service speaks only voice cues of breakdowns it served (by key), audio is
    cached by key, requests are rate-limited per client (`TTE_TTS_RATE_LIMIT`) and syntheses are
    capped per UTC day (`TTE_DAILY_TTS_CAP`; a failed synthesis gives its unit back, and requests
    for one key share a single synthesis). At most `TTE_TTS_CONCURRENCY` Azure calls run at once.
    The template lines bill about 80 characters each (markup counts, a Chinese character twice),
    so 200 a day for 22 days is about 350K, under F0's 500K a month.
  - Per-client limits key on the last `X-Forwarded-For` hop, the address the Container Apps
    ingress appends; earlier hops come from the client. Live streams also have a ceiling per
    process (`TTE_LIVE_MAX_TOTAL`), and uvicorn refuses WebSocket frames over 4 KiB.
  - Egress: every response body and live message counts against a per-client budget
    (`TTE_EGRESS_PER_CLIENT`, 500 MB an hour) and a daily total (`TTE_DAILY_EGRESS_GB`, 10 GB),
    since Azure bills internet egress past 100 GB a month; the live stream plays at most 8x. At
    most `TTE_CREW_CONCURRENCY` crew runs with a model at once (each starts an MCP subprocess).
    The audit, a local load test and what happens when a quota runs out: docs/OPERATIONS.md.
  - Judging window (`TTE_JUDGING_WINDOW`, 2026-10-27 to 2026-11-10, UTC days): 8 model runs per
    client per 10 min and 100 a day, so a judge's tour (what-if, drill, bring-back, recap) fits,
    two judges behind one NAT too. A limited drill or bring-back replays the recording of the
    same run, labelled with its time, or runs without the model: never a dead end.
  - Log Analytics has a daily ingestion cap below the free 5 GB/month. An Azure budget alert is set
    at $40 if the offer supports budgets.
